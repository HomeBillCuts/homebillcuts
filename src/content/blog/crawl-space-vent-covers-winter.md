Open foundation vents keep a **vented crawlspace** breathing in summer — and can dump **outdoor winter air** under your floors all heating season. Cold washes chill joists, ducts, and water lines; rooms above feel drafty even when the thermostat works. **Seasonal crawl space / foundation vent covers** (foam plugs, slide-damper replacement vents, region-specific vanity covers, or DIY foam/Reflectix inserts) close those openings until spring. This guide is **bill-first**: measure each vent, respect flood-vent and moisture limits, pick plug vs damper vs DIY vs skip, remove covers in spring, and never confuse a foam plug with **crawlspace encapsulation**. No guaranteed dollar savings.

## Honest framing: seasonal air control under the floor — not encapsulation

Vented crawlspaces were built so outdoor air could dilute moisture in warmer months. In heating season that same air is often **the wrong air**: cold, wind-driven, and expensive under the floor.

**Covers help when:** you have **standard screened foundation vents** into a vented crawl; winters chill floors/ducts/pipes through those openings; you will **remove plugs or open dampers in spring**; and you are not blocking **flood vents** or combustion air. Treat the purchase as **seasonal draft control**, not whole-house R-value.

**Covers do less (or the wrong thing) when:** the crawl is already **encapsulated**; vents are **flood vents**; you have **active water or mold**; code requires vents open; or you leave foam in all summer and trap moisture.

**We do not guarantee savings.** Closing leaky vents can cut under-floor heat loss — or feel like theater if the rim joist and garage still roar. Climate, rates, floor insulation, and habits decide. Same seasonal ritual family as [outdoor faucet covers](/blog/outdoor-faucet-covers-freeze-protection/) and [window AC winter covers](/blog/window-ac-winter-covers-draft-sealing/).

## Measure-first walk: count vents, measure openings, decide (before you buy)

Do this once on a cool day before you cart a multipack of foam:

1. **Count every foundation / crawl vent** — walk the perimeter (including behind shrubs/decks). Photograph each size class and damaged screens.
2. **Confirm vented crawl vs encapsulated** — bare dirt or old plastic with open vents → traditionally vented (this guide). Sealed liner + designed drying/conditioning + vents already blocked as a system → **encapsulation path**; don’t randomly add Frost King plugs.
3. **Spot flood vents and must-stay-open openings** — FEMA/flood vents and combustion-air openings are **not** foam-plug candidates. When unsure, leave them alone and ask a pro or building department.
4. **Measure inside opening W × H** — tape the **clear inside** of the vent frame (example classes: ~5.5″×7.75″ small foam, ~6.5″×7.75″ large foam, ~15 7/8″×7 1/4″ vanity class). Not the brick border.
5. **Note center mullion / vertical panel** — a center bar may need one plug per bay, or a vanity cover that expects that panel. Write it down per vent.
6. **Note rough opening for replacement dampers** — if you’ll swap the whole vent, measure the masonry/rough opening. Many damper vents are **16″×8″** class — verify on *your* wall before a 3-pack.
7. **Crawl moisture reality check** — standing water, mold on joists, heavy must, or sodden insulation → water management / vapor barrier **before** sealing winter air paths. Covers do not pump water out.
8. **Decide plug vs damper vs vanity vs DIY vs skip** — spring-removable rectangular screens → Frost King–class foam. Want open/close without storing foam → slide-damper vents. OR/WA/ID/MT with exact vanity opening + center panel → vanity + PE foam. Odd shapes → DIY Reflectix/rigid foam labeled for spring. Flood vents / encapsulated / chronic water → skip.

If **pipes freeze** in the same crawl, measure those runs for [insulation](/blog/insulate-hot-water-pipes-water-heater-jacket/) and optional [heat tape](/blog/pipe-freeze-heat-tape-protection/) after you stop the wind wash — covers and pipe protection often stack.

## Situation priorities + do-not list

### Prioritize seasonal vent covers when

1. **Open screened foundation vents** ring a vented crawl in a cold or mixed climate.
2. **Cold floors, chilly ducts, or freeze-prone pipes** sit over that crawl with vents open all winter.
3. **House-side leaks already handled** ([weatherstripping](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/), [garage seals](/blog/garage-door-weather-seals-bottom-seals/), [checklist](/blog/lower-electric-bill-priority-checklist/)) and the remaining wash is under-floor outdoor air.
4. **You’ll remove plugs / open dampers in spring** — same calendar week as faucet covers.
5. **Renters with permission** — friction-fit foam is reversible; don’t alter flood or combustion openings.

### Do-not list (moisture + safety + wasted money)

- **Never seal FEMA / flood vents** with foam, screws, or silicone.
- **Never block combustion make-up air** or dryer/appliance vents “for winter.”
- **Don’t leave foam plugs in all summer** on a traditionally vented crawl — reopen to dry; mold is the honest downside of year-round sealing without a moisture plan.
- **Don’t treat plugs as encapsulation** — that means sealed ground cover, air sealing, and usually mechanical drying. Different project.
- **Don’t foam over standing water or ignore mold** — fix water first.
- **Don’t buy one size for every opening** — houses mix frames; measure.
- **Don’t assume a PNW vanity cover fits a Midwest 8×16 screen** — needs ~15 7/8″×7 1/4″ inside **and** a vertical center panel; shortlist combo is for **OR/WA/ID/MT**.
- **Don’t invent guaranteed bill cuts** from a product title.
- **Don’t glue plugs so hard you destroy the frame** — friction fit beats permanent adhesive on a seasonal product.

## Buyer decision tree (plug vs damper vs vanity vs DIY vs skip)

Walk the tree in order:

1. **Traditionally vented crawl with screened foundation vents?**  
   No (encapsulated / no vents / slab) → stop. See [air sealing](/blog/air-sealing-caulk-foam-outlet-gaskets/) or [pipe insulation](/blog/insulate-hot-water-pipes-water-heater-jacket/) instead.  
   Yes → continue.

2. **Any flood vents or required-open combustion/flood hardware?**  
   Yes on those openings → **skip covers there**; only treat ordinary screens.  
   All ordinary screens → continue.

3. **Standing water, active mold, or swamp smell?**  
   Yes → **pause the cart**; grade, gutters, drainage, vapor barrier / pro first. Plugs on a wet crawl trade problems.  
   Dry enough → continue.

4. **Cheapest reversible winter close, pull foam in spring?**  
   Yes → **Frost King–class foam plugs** sized to each inside opening (small ~5.5″×7.75″ vs large ~6.5″×7.75″ — match *your* tape).  
   Want built-in open/close without storing foam → continue.

5. **Rough opening matches a slide-damper vent (often 16″×8″) and you’ll swap the old screen?**  
   Yes → **damper + screen replacement** (e.g. JIANBENKJ-class 16×8 multipack). Close winter, open spring.  
   Odd masonry → foam plugs or DIY.

6. **OR / WA / ID / MT with ~15 7/8″ × 7 1/4″ inside opening and a vertical center panel?**  
   Yes → **Vanity Vents–class cover + 2″ PE foam** — confirm listing region and dimensions.  
   No / wrong size / no center panel → do not force that ASIN; use plugs, dampers, or DIY.

7. **Odd shapes, damaged frames, or one-offs?**  
   **DIY inserts** from [Reflectix](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0022NH3E4/?tag=homebillcuts-20) and/or home-center rigid foam, friction-fit, **labeled “REMOVE IN SPRING.”**  
   Still unsure → plug the worst windward vents with one correct foam size, live a cold week, then finish the ring.

### Type / size notes (verify before checkout)

| Approach | What it is | Fit notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Small foam plug (~5.5″×7.75″ class)** | Expanded foam insert (Frost King / Thermwell VP57-class) | Friction-fit into smaller rectangular screened openings; measure inside W×H |
| **Large foam plug (~6.5″×7.75″ class)** | Larger foam insert (Frost King VP67-class / “FOUNDTN VENT WHT L 7.75″”) | Same idea for bigger frames — don’t force a small plug into a large hole |
| **Slide-damper + screen vent (16″×8″ rough class)** | Replacement foundation vent with adjustable damper | Match rough/masonry opening; black multipacks common for whole-perimeter swaps |
| **Vanity cover + PE foam (PNW)** | Decorative cover + ~2″ PE foam insert | Needs ~15 7/8″×7 1/4″ inside opening **and** vertical center panel; shortlist combo aimed at **OR/WA/ID/MT** |
| **DIY Reflectix / rigid foam** | Cut-to-fit inserts | Odd sizes; label and remove in spring; tape seams thoughtfully — don’t glue permanently |
| **Skip / different project** | No cover | Flood vents, encapsulation systems, wet crawls, or code-required open vents |

**Buy for the opening you measured.** A “universal” plug that falls out or leaves a 1″ gap is comfort theater. When openings are split by a center mullion, plan **two** small inserts or a product that expects that bar — don’t pretend one undersized plug covers both bays.

## Install / use troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plug falls out or rattles | Undersized foam; smooth painted frame; wind pressure | Upsize to the correct W×H class; light removable foam tape gasket if maker-safe; don’t use permanent construction adhesive on a seasonal plug |
| Still feel cold floors | Other vents left open; rim-joist leaks; bare ducts; weak floor insulation | Finish the vent ring; air-seal rim ([air sealing](/blog/air-sealing-caulk-foam-outlet-gaskets/)); insulate ducts/floors when accessible |
| Condensation or musty smell after sealing | Moisture source; plugs left into warm season; no ground cover | Open vents in spring; fix grading/gutters/standing water; vapor barrier conversation; don’t run a sealed wet crawl all year on foam alone |
| Damper won’t slide / screen bent | Paint/debris; wrong rough opening; warped frame | Clean track; confirm 16×8-class fit; adjust or replace |
| Vanity cover doesn’t sit | Wrong inside dimensions; missing center panel; wrong region kit | Remeasure 15 7/8″×7 1/4″ + mullion; return if geometry doesn’t match; use foam plugs or DIY instead |
| Pipes still froze | Vent covers helped air wash but lines stayed wet/exposed | [Insulate pipes](/blog/insulate-hot-water-pipes-water-heater-jacket/); listed [heat tape](/blog/pipe-freeze-heat-tape-protection/) if needed; drain outdoor bibs ([faucet covers](/blog/outdoor-faucet-covers-freeze-protection/)) |
| Pest chewed foam | Open attractant; soft foam at grade | Repair screen/damper; replace plug; address gaps at access doors |
| HOA / flood-zone notice | Covered a flood vent | **Remove immediately**; restore flood-vent function; only cover ordinary screens |
| Renter conflict at move-out | Covers left in / frames damaged | Pull all seasonal plugs; document; avoid permanent adhesives |

## Honest limits (what a vent cover will not fix)

- A **wet crawl**, bad grading/gutters, or missing ground vapor barrier.
- **Guaranteed** heating-bill cuts or catalog R-value as your whole-house result.
- **Flood-vent** compliance or life-safety openings.
- Full **encapsulation** (sealed liner, mechanical drying, rim air sealing as a system).
- **Bare ducts and uninsulated floors** that still lose heat after vents are quiet.
- **Rim-joist and garage** washes that bypass crawl vents ([garage seals](/blog/garage-door-weather-seals-bottom-seals/), [weatherstripping](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/)).
- **Combustion / CO** problems — detectors and licensed service, not foam.
- Pipes that still need [heat tape](/blog/pipe-freeze-heat-tape-protection/) after insulation — covers are not heat cable.

## Post-setup checklist + habits

1. Confirm every covered opening is an **ordinary screened foundation vent**, not flood or combustion.
2. Log **size class per vent** (photo + W×H) for next fall’s order.
3. Install foam snug but removable; close dampers fully; seat vanity/DIY inserts without wrecking frames.
4. After the **first hard freeze night**, walk the perimeter and reseat anything that shifted.
5. Pair with [pipe insulation](/blog/insulate-hot-water-pipes-water-heater-jacket/), optional [heat tape](/blog/pipe-freeze-heat-tape-protection/), [faucet winterize](/blog/outdoor-faucet-covers-freeze-protection/).
6. Weatherstrip the **access door** so you didn’t seal vents and leave a barn door open.
7. **Spring reminder**: pull foam, open dampers, store plugs dry, check screens for pests.
8. Two winters of must/wet wood → stop treating it as a plug problem; call a crawl / home-performance pro.

### Habits that multiply (or replace) a cover purchase

- Fix gutters and grade so meltwater isn’t feeding the crawl you’re trying to calm.
- Seal the house above: [weatherstripping](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/), [air sealing](/blog/air-sealing-caulk-foam-outlet-gaskets/), [garage seals](/blog/garage-door-weather-seals-bottom-seals/).
- Don’t “encapsulate” with random plastic, closed vents, and no drying plan.
- Keep one spare correctly sized foam plug; foam gets brittle and pests chew.
- Track floor comfort and freeze incidents separately from kWh fantasies — covers can be worth it as insurance even when the meter barely moves.

### Will crawl space vent covers lower my heating bill?

### Is it safe to seal crawl space vents permanently?
