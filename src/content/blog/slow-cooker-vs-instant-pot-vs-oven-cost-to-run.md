## Why the appliances differ

Every braise needs the food brought up to a simmer and held there. What you pay for is everything *else*:

- **An oven** heats a large insulated box (often 4–5 cubic feet) plus racks and walls, then loses heat through the door, vent, and every peek. Bake elements are typically 2,000–3,500 W while on.
- **A slow cooker** heats only a ceramic crock and a lid sitting on the food, with a small element — often around 150–300 W on low and higher on high.
- **An electric pressure cooker** (Instant Pot and similar) uses a ~700–1,000 W element, but a sealed pot under pressure cooks at about 240–250°F, so the dish is done in a fraction of the time.
- **A stovetop pot** depends on your burner and lid: a lidded Dutch oven on a low gas or induction flame can be cheap; an uncovered pot on a big coil is not.

Read the table honestly: switching one weekly oven braise to a slow cooker or pressure cooker is roughly **$10–25 a year**. Real, but small. If you run the oven anyway (baking bread, roasting vegetables), adding the stew pot to the same session costs very little extra — **batching beats appliance shopping**.

Gas looks cheap here because a low simmer needs little heat and gas often costs less per unit of heat. Use your own price per therm, and run the range hood — burners release combustion byproducts into the room.

## Measure first (~5 steps)

1. **Find your all-in rate.** Bill total for electricity ÷ kWh used (energy + delivery). Do the same per therm if you have gas.
2. **Meter one slow-cooker or pressure-cooker cook.** Plug it into a [Kill A Watt](/blog/kill-a-watt-plug-in-energy-meter/), reset it, cook your usual recipe, and read total kWh at the end (include any warm time you normally use).
3. **Estimate the oven.** Most ovens are 240 V or hardwired, so a plug meter won't fit. Use your utility's hourly smart-meter data at a quiet time, or a circuit monitor like [Emporia or Sense](/blog/emporia-vue-vs-sense/).
4. **Multiply by how often you cook.** kWh per batch × batches per month × rate = your actual monthly cost for this habit.
5. **Compare to your big loads.** If that number is a dollar or two a month, your money is better spent on heating, drafts, and hot water first.

## Free fixes before you buy anything

- **Don't preheat the oven for a braise longer than needed**, and don't open the door to check — each peek dumps heat.
- **Batch oven cooking.** If the oven is on, fill it: stew pot, roasted vegetables, and tomorrow's lunch together.
- **Keep the slow cooker lid on.** Lifting it adds cook time; many manufacturers suggest roughly 15–30 extra minutes per peek.
- **Don't leave food on warm for hours** after it's done. It adds kWh and degrades texture; refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.
- **Use a lid on the stovetop** and the lowest flame that holds a simmer.
- **Fill to the right level.** Slow cookers work best about half to two-thirds full; a nearly empty large crock can overcook, and an overfilled one may heat too slowly for safety.
- **Use what you own.** A stovetop pressure cooker or heavy lidded Dutch oven already gets you most of the savings.

## Food safety (non-negotiable)

- **Thaw meat and poultry in the refrigerator before slow cooking** (USDA guidance). Frozen chunks can linger in the 40–140°F danger zone.
- **Dried red kidney beans must be boiled** (about 10 minutes at a rolling boil) before slow cooking, or use canned beans; low slow-cooker temperatures may not destroy the natural toxin.
- **Check doneness with a food thermometer:** 165°F for poultry, 145°F plus rest for whole cuts of beef/pork, 160°F for ground meat.
- **Pressure cookers:** follow the manual's minimum liquid, max-fill line, and release instructions; never force the lid open.

## Should you buy one? A quick decision tree

- **You braise in the oven weekly and own neither:** a basic 6–7 qt slow cooker is the cheapest way in. A programmable model that switches to warm when done helps if you work long days.
- **You want weeknight speed (beans, stock, rice, stews in an hour):** an electric pressure cooker that also has a slow-cook mode replaces both.
- **You cook for one or two:** a 3-quart mini pressure cooker uses less counter space and heats less mass.
- **You already own a Dutch oven and gas or induction:** you probably don't need anything new.

## Safety do-nots

- **Don't run a slow cooker or pressure cooker on an extension cord or power strip.** Plug directly into a wall outlet.
- **Keep cords and the appliance away from counter edges** and off the stovetop.
- **Don't leave a gas burner unattended**, and use the hood.
- **Don't put a cold stoneware crock onto a hot base or into a hot oven**; sudden temperature changes can crack it.
