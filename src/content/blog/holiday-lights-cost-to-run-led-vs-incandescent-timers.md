Holiday lights are one of the few seasonal loads you control completely: you pick the bulbs, the count, and the hours. **The bill is just watts × hours × your rate**, so the two levers are lower-watt lights and fewer hours on. This guide is **bill-first**: do the math for your own display, fix the free hours problem with a timer, and only replace strings where the numbers say it pays. No guaranteed savings.

## The formula

**kWh = watts × hours on ÷ 1,000.** Cost = kWh × your all-in rate (bill total for electricity ÷ kWh used). Total watts is on the string's tag or box; add every string, inflatable, and projector.

## Example season math (illustrative, $0.17/kWh, 40 nights)

| Item (typical label watts) | 6 hr/night (timer) | 24 hr/day (left on) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LED mini string, 100 bulbs (~5–7 W) | ~1.4 kWh, ~25¢ | ~5.8 kWh, ~$1 |
| Incandescent mini string, 100 bulbs (~40 W) | ~9.6 kWh, ~$1.65 | ~38 kWh, ~$6.50 |
| Incandescent C9 string, 25 bulbs (~7 W each, ~175 W) | ~42 kWh, ~$7 | ~168 kWh, ~$29 |
| LED C9 string, 25 bulbs (often ~15–25 W total) | ~5 kWh, ~85¢ | ~19 kWh, ~$3.30 |
| Blower inflatable (~150 W) | ~36 kWh, ~$6 | ~144 kWh, ~$24.50 |

Read the table honestly: one LED mini string is pocket change either way. The money is in **incandescent C7/C9 strings, inflatables, and 24/7 hours**. A big mixed display left on all night can reach tens of dollars a season; the same display on a timer is often a quarter of that.

## Measure first (~4 steps)

1. **Read the tags.** Every UL-listed string lists watts or amps (watts ≈ amps × 120).
2. **Meter what you can't read.** Plug a string or inflatable into a [Kill A Watt](/blog/kill-a-watt-plug-in-energy-meter/) for an hour and read kWh.
3. **Count your hours.** Dusk to 11pm is about 6 hours in December; "always on" is 24.
4. **Add it up** and multiply by your rate. Now you know whether anything is worth replacing.

## Free and cheap fixes, in order

1. **Schedule it.** A dusk-to-bedtime schedule is the biggest single cut and works with lights you already own. Outdoor-rated timers or outdoor smart plugs only.
2. **Unplug inflatables overnight** — fans run constantly.
3. **Replace only the heavy hitters.** Swap incandescent C7/C9 and big incandescent runs first; leave working LED strings alone.
4. **Don't buy more than you'll run.** The cheapest watt is the string you skip.

## When buying makes sense

- **You have no timer and run lights 10+ hours a night:** an outdoor timer or outdoor smart plug pays back in one season on most displays bigger than a couple of strings.
- **You run incandescent C9s or lots of incandescent minis:** LED replacements usually pay back in one to three seasons depending on hours.
- **You already run LED on a schedule:** you're done. Spend the effort on [heating and drafts](/start-here/) instead.

## Safety do-nots

- No indoor plugs, timers, or extension cords outdoors. Use outdoor-rated gear on **GFCI** outlets.
- Don't exceed the max-connect number printed on a string, or the plug's watt/amp rating.
- Don't staple through wires; use plastic clips. Keep connections off the ground and out of snow and puddles.
- Throw out strings with cracked insulation or broken sockets. Don't run cords through closed doors or windows where they get pinched.
- Turn displays off when you're away or asleep if they aren't on a timer.
