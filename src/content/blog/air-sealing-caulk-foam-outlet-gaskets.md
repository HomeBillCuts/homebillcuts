Heating season is when leaky envelopes show up on the bill: conditioned air escapes through tiny holes, and your furnace or heat pump works harder to keep up. **Caulk, low-expansion foam, foam backer rod, and outlet/switch gaskets** are the cheap sealing kit that sits *beside* door sweeps and window film — not a replacement for either. This guide is **bill-first**: where to look, which product for which gap, safety limits, and a short Amazon list — without inventing dollar savings.

## Why small holes show up on winter bills

Air moves from high pressure to low. Stack effect (warm air rising and escaping high in the house) plus wind pressure pull outdoor air in through baseboards, outlets on exterior walls, cable/pipe penetrations, and attic hatch gaps. Every cubic foot you heat and then lose is waste — not because a single outlet “costs $50,” but because **many small leaks add runtime**.

**Honest framing:** Savings scale with how leaky the house was, your climate, fuel and rates, and whether you sealed *meaningful* paths (not decorative bead lines on tight new construction). We do **not** guarantee a dollar cut. Energy agencies often cite air leakage as a large share of heating/cooling loss in typical US stock — treat that as motivation to inspect, not a promise for your address.

## Caulk vs foam vs gaskets vs backer rod

| Gap type | Better tool |
| --- | --- |
| Hairline cracks at trim, baseboards, window/door casing (stationary joints) | Paintable acrylic latex caulk (+ silicone) |
| Gaps ~¼″–1″ around pipes, wires, dryer vents (non-window frames) | Minimal-expanding “gaps & cracks” foam |
| Rough openings around windows/doors (trim removed or new install) | **Window & door** low-pressure foam (won’t bow frames) |
| Deep joints before caulk (control depth, save sealant) | Closed-cell foam backer rod |
| Drafts at exterior-wall outlets and switches | Foam gaskets behind cover plates |
| Moving door bottoms / sash edges | Sweeps, V-strip, rope caulk — [weatherstripping](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/) |
| Whole window face / cold glass rooms | [Shrink film](/blog/window-insulation-kits-winter-drafts/) |

**Rule of thumb:** caulk for narrow static cracks; foam for irregular voids; gaskets for plates you can unscrew in five minutes; weatherstrip for parts that move.

## Where to look (30-minute draft hunt)

Do this on a cool, breezy day or with HVAC running so pressure differences are obvious. A stick of incense or a damp hand works as a cheap detector.

1. **Exterior-wall outlets and switches** — feel for air with the plate slightly loose; prioritize rooms that never hold setpoint.
2. **Baseboards and shoe molding** on exterior walls, especially older plaster or remodeled rooms.
3. **Cable, coax, phone, and pipe penetrations** through floors, rim joists, and exterior walls (basement/crawlspace and attic sides count).
4. **Dryer, bath, and kitchen vent boots** where duct meets wall or ceiling — seal the *perimeter*, don’t crush the duct.
5. **Attic hatch / pull-down stairs** perimeter — often a large leak; weatherstrip the lid and seal the frame gap.
6. **Behind kitchen and bath cabinets** on exterior walls — plumbing and wiring holes are common.
7. **Can lights and ceiling fixtures** into unconditioned attics (careful: use rated seal methods; don’t bury non-IC fixtures in foam).

Skip obsessing over interior partitions that don’t touch outdoors or attics — those rarely move the bill.

## Safety first (read before you spray)

- **Combustion appliances** (furnaces, water heaters, fireplaces, gas dryers) need make-up air. Do not seal every crack in a room that houses an atmospherically vented appliance without understanding venting. If you smell combustion odors or get frequent pilot outages after sealing, stop and call a pro.
- **Electrical boxes:** Foam and caulk belong *around* the box / between box and drywall — not packed inside the box volume. Keep product off terminations. Kill power at the breaker before removing plates if you’re unsure.
- **Fireblocking / rated assemblies:** Some walls and penetrations require listed firestop products. Ordinary foam is for air sealing, not a substitute for firestopping where code requires it.
- **Windows and doors:** Use **window & door** (low-pressure / low-expansion) foam in rough openings. High-expansion “gaps & cracks” foam can bow frames.
- **Egress:** Never permanently seal bedroom windows you need for escape. Film kits and caulk on non-egress trim are fine; blocking operable egress is not.
- **Foam mess:** Wear gloves and eye protection. Cured foam trims with a knife; skin cleanup is miserable — prevent rather than scrub.

## DIY order that respects the bill

1. Finish free wins from the [checklist](/blog/lower-electric-bill-priority-checklist/) (setpoints, filters, obvious habits).
2. Fix **door sweeps and sash weatherstrip** you can feel ([weatherstripping](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/)).
3. Install **outlet/switch gaskets** on exterior walls — fast, reversible, renter-friendly.
4. Caulk obvious baseboard and trim cracks on exterior walls.
5. Foam or backer-rod + caulk larger penetrations in basements, rim joists, and attics.
6. Add seasonal [window film](/blog/window-insulation-kits-winter-drafts/) and [thermal curtains](/blog/thermal-blackout-curtains-energy-savings/) where glass still feels cold.
7. Only then plan bigger attic air-sealing / insulation ([attic guide](/blog/attic-insulation-approaches-when-to-call-pro/)).

## When air sealing helps *your* bill

It can help when:

- You feel air at outlets, baseboards, or penetrations on exterior walls.
- Rooms near the envelope never hold setpoint despite sane thermostat schedules ([smart thermostat guide](/blog/best-smart-thermostats-2026/)).
- You’re heading into heating season and haven’t sealed obvious holes yet.
- Utility bills spike with windy cold snaps more than with mild cold — a classic infiltration signature.

It usually won’t move the needle much when:

- The house is already tight new construction with continuous air barriers.
- The real driver is a failed HVAC system, electric strip heat thrashing, or an empty attic (fix depth and bypasses first).
- You only caulk cosmetic interior trim that never sees outdoors.
- Combustion or moisture problems need a pro — sealing over symptoms can make those worse.

## Bottom line

Before winter, spend an afternoon on **gaskets, caulk, and the right foam for the gap** — then layer door weatherstrip and window film where glass and moving parts still leak. Keep foam out of electrical-box interiors, use window-rated foam on frames, and don’t starve combustion appliances of air.
