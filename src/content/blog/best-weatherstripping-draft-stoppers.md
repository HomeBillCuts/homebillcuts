Drafty doors and windows waste conditioned air and make HVAC run longer. **Weatherstripping and door draft stoppers** are among the cheapest fixes on the [priority checklist](/blog/lower-electric-bill-priority-checklist/) — but only if you seal the right gaps and keep safety/egress in mind. This guide is **bill-first**: walk the house before you buy a kit, prioritize exterior doors (and garage-to-house), pick sweep vs snake vs V-strip vs rope caulk vs full door replacement, and install so seals actually hold — without inventing dollar savings.

## When drafts actually move your bill

Sealing helps most when:

- You feel obvious air movement at exterior door bottoms, thresholds, or window sashes.
- Rooms nearest exterior doors never hold temperature despite HVAC running.
- Winter heating or summer A/C runtime is high *and* the envelope is leaky (common in older US stock).
- You already fixed setpoints and dirty [HVAC filters](/blog/hvac-air-filters-merv-efficiency/) — drafts are the next cheap lever.

Sealing helps less when:

- The real problem is failed HVAC, undersized equipment, or electric strip heat thrashing.
- You’re only sealing interior doors (comfort maybe; bill impact usually small).
- Moisture / ice dams / combustion-appliance air supply need professional diagnosis first.
- You expect tape and foam alone to match a full insulation retrofit or a wide-open attic (see [attic insulation](/blog/attic-insulation-approaches-when-to-call-pro/)).

**Honest framing:** Weatherization reduces *infiltration*. Savings scale with how leaky the home was, your climate, and your rates — not with the brand printed on the packaging. We do **not** guarantee a dollar cut. Treat seals as an envelope step, not a substitute for attic air sealing or a failed heating system.

## Measure first: walk the house before you buy a kit

Ordering a whole-house carton of foam tape before a 15-minute walk is how you end up with the wrong profile stuck to dirty jambs and a sweep that scrapes the floor. Do this once:

1. **Incense or tissue test** — on a windy day or while HVAC is running, hold a stick of incense or a thin tissue near door edges, window locks, attic hatches, and cable penetrations. Note where the smoke or tissue *moves*.
2. **Daylight gaps** — check under exterior doors from outside with lights off inside (or vice versa). A visible strip of light usually means the sweep or threshold needs work.
3. **Label doors** — note *which* doors are true exterior, which are garage-to-house, and which are interior only. Garage-entry doors often need a heavier seal than people think; interior bedroom doors rarely move the bill.
4. **Photograph thresholds and jambs** — snap the bottom, the existing sweep style (screw-on, adhesive, door-bottom with drip cap), and any flattened or cracked foam/V-strip. Buy the profile that matches the gap, not the prettiest listing photo.
5. **Gap sizing** — estimate under-door clearance (cardboard shim or coin stack) and side/top compression. Match compressed weatherstrip thickness to the gap; oversized foam crushes flat in one season, undersized leaves a whistle.
6. **Safety pass** — combustion air, dryer vents, egress, CO/gas (see do-not-seal list below). Safety beats a cozy draft fix.

If you smell gas, see scorched outlets, or suspect carbon-monoxide / combustion-air issues, stop DIY sealing around those appliances and call a qualified pro. If cold *glass* is the villain more than edge air, jump to [window insulation kits](/blog/window-insulation-kits-winter-drafts/) or [thermal curtains](/blog/thermal-blackout-curtains-energy-savings/) after you fix any daylight door gap.

## Door and location priorities (what to seal first)

### Prioritize (biggest comfort + bill leverage)

1. **Main exterior entry with a daylight bottom gap** — highest traffic and often the worst under-door leak. Sweep/threshold first.
2. **Garage-to-house door** — attached garages dump cold (and sometimes exhaust/fumes) into living space; treat this like an exterior door.
3. **Back / side exterior doors** used daily — same stack: bottom, then perimeter.
4. **Sliding glass patio doors** — track pile + clean/lube often beats buying another fabric snake; pair with [shrink film](/blog/window-insulation-kits-winter-drafts/) if the glass still howls.
5. **Leaky double-hung sash stops** you can feel with incense — V-strip or seasonal rope caulk after doors are done.

### Lower priority / skip for now

- Interior closet and hallway doors (comfort only); already-tight modern doors with intact factory weatherstrip; guest-room windows you never occupy in heating season.

### Do-not-seal safety list

- **Combustion air** — never block required make-up air for furnaces, water heaters, or fireplaces.
- **Dryer vents, range hoods, bathroom fans** — do not tape or foam them closed.
- **Bedroom egress** — keep operable exit windows and doors usable; don’t immobilize the only exit with permanent seals you can’t open fast.
- **Gas smell / CO concerns** — stop DIY sealing and call a qualified pro.
- **Flame / heat sources** — use flame-resistant materials near heat per product labels; keep fabric draft snakes clear of space heaters.
- **Knob-and-tube or damaged wiring** in cavities you’re about to foam — consult an electrician before filling voids.
- **Ladder work on exterior casing** — fall awareness; don’t chase a $20 seal into a hospital bill.

Seal the **worst two exterior doors** first, live through a cold windy night, then decide if windows and secondary doors are worth the next kit.

## Buyer decision tree (sweep vs snake vs V-strip vs rope caulk vs new door)

Use this before you cart six kinds of foam:

1. **Daylight under an exterior door you own?** → **Screw-on sweep / threshold kit** (or door-bottom shoe). Measure thickness and swing clearance so it doesn’t bind on flooring.
2. **Renter, temporary, or can’t screw into the slab?** → **Weighted fabric draft snake** under the door + removable **rope caulk** on leaky sash gaps. Expect a partial fix — snakes don’t seal side jambs.
3. **Side and top gaps; smoke moves at the stop?** → **V-strip / spring bronze or quality silicone kerf weatherstrip**. Match compressed thickness. Clean with isopropyl before adhesive products.
4. **Seasonal window sash you want open again in spring?** → **Removable rope caulk** (Mortite-style). Warm in your hands if the room is cold.
5. **Sliding glass track feels drafty?** → Clean + lube the track; replace worn **pile weatherstrip** before buying another snake.
6. **Door slab warped, delaminated, or missing a proper threshold stack?** → Kits won’t fix a banana-shaped door. Quote an **insulated prehung exterior door** only after kits fail — verify handing, jamb depth, and rough opening.
7. **Still undecided vs other tools?** → Cold glass face → [shrink film](/blog/window-insulation-kits-winter-drafts/). Night coverage / privacy → [thermal curtains](/blog/thermal-blackout-curtains-energy-savings/). Fixed cracks / outlets → [air sealing](/blog/air-sealing-caulk-foam-outlet-gaskets/). Attic hatch dump → [attic stair covers](/blog/attic-stair-hatch-insulation-covers/). Overhead garage → [garage seals](/blog/garage-door-weather-seals-bottom-seals/).

**Measure tip:** Photograph the existing seal and estimate gap height before you buy. Listing photos rarely show your threshold.

| Big holes / gaps | Backer rod + caulk, or low-expansion foam carefully | Low-expansion only near frames — foam can bow jambs |

## Install / troubleshooting table

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sweep binds / scrapes flooring | Sweep set too low; uneven slab; thick rug | Raise or trim sweep; check threshold height; planing may need a pro |
| Adhesive foam / V-strip peels in a week | Dirty, damp, or silicone-contaminated surface | Clean with isopropyl; dry fully; fit dry before peeling backing; press firmly |
| Still drafty at the sides after a snake | Snake only covers the bottom | Add V-strip or screw-on jamb seals on sides/top; snakes are partial |
| Daylight still under the door | Worn threshold; wrong sweep profile; warped slab | Adjust/replace threshold; try a door-bottom shoe; warped slab → door replacement path |
| Sliding door still howls | Dirty track; worn pile; misaligned panels | Vacuum/lube track; replace pile weatherstrip; don’t expect a fabric snake to seal a patio track |
| Cheap open-cell foam flattened by mid-winter | Wrong material for friction/sun | Switch to V-strip, kerf silicone, or closed-cell / EPDM profiles that rebound |
| Door hard to latch after new strip | Weatherstrip too thick at rest | Use thinner profile; compress without crushing flat; adjust strike plate carefully |
| Rope caulk crumbly / won’t stick | Too cold / old stock | Warm between palms; press firmly; replace if dried out |
| Garage-to-house door still cold | Light foam only; missing sweep | Treat like exterior: real sweep + perimeter seal; check weatherstrip on the overhead garage door separately |

Recheck after a week of seasonal humidity swings; retighten sweep screws. Combine with the free habits in the [electric bill checklist](/blog/lower-electric-bill-priority-checklist/) — sealing a door while running A/C at 68°F with dirty filters still wastes money.

## Install habits that keep seals working

1. Clean the surface; adhesive hates dust and silicone residue.
2. Fit dry before peeling adhesive backing.
3. Compress weatherstrip firmly without crushing it flat at rest.
4. Recheck after a week of seasonal humidity swings; retighten sweep screws.
5. Combine with the free habits in the [electric bill checklist](/blog/lower-electric-bill-priority-checklist/) — sealing a door while running A/C at 68°F with dirty filters still wastes money.

## FAQ

### Does weatherstripping really lower heating bills?

It can when you seal real exterior-door and window infiltration that makes HVAC run longer. Savings scale with how leaky the home was, your climate, and your rates—not the brand on the package. We do not promise a specific dollar cut.

### Where should I start sealing drafts for winter?

Start with visible exterior-door bottom gaps (sweep/threshold), then perimeters with V-strip or quality silicone kerf weatherstrip. Attic pull-down stairs and attached garage doors often need dedicated seals beyond a fabric draft snake.

### Are door draft snakes enough for leaky exterior doors?

They’re a temporary or renter-friendly under-door helper, not a full seal. They don’t fix side jambs. If you own the door, a real sweep/threshold fix usually lasts longer.

### Is weatherstripping safe around furnaces and fireplaces?

Never block required combustion air for furnaces, water heaters, or fireplaces. Don’t seal dryer vents, range hoods, or bathroom fans closed. Keep bedroom egress windows operable. If you smell gas or suspect CO issues, stop DIY sealing and call a qualified pro.

### Should I weatherstrip, use window film, or hang curtains first?

Use weatherstripping for door bottoms and sash/stop edge gaps. Use indoor shrink-film kits for a seasonal seal across leaky glass. Use thermal curtains for daily open/close coverage. Stack tools when both edges and glass leak—don’t film an egress bedroom shut while people sleep there.
