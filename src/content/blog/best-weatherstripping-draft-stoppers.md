Drafty doors and windows waste conditioned air and make HVAC run longer. Weatherstripping and door draft stoppers are among the cheapest fixes on the [priority checklist](/blog/lower-electric-bill-priority-checklist/) — but only if you seal the right gaps and keep safety/egress in mind. This guide is for typical US homes, not a promise of a specific dollar cut on your next bill.

## When drafts actually move your bill

Sealing helps most when:

- You feel obvious air movement at exterior door bottoms, thresholds, or window sashes.
- Rooms nearest exterior doors never hold temperature despite HVAC running.
- Winter heating or summer A/C runtime is high *and* the envelope is leaky (common in older US stock).
- You already fixed setpoints and filters — drafts are the next cheap lever.

Sealing helps less when:

- The real problem is failed HVAC, undersized equipment, or electric strip heat thrashing.
- You’re only sealing interior doors (comfort maybe; bill impact usually small).
- Moisture / ice dams / combustion-appliance air supply need professional diagnosis first.
- You expect tape and foam alone to match a full insulation retrofit.

**Honest framing:** Weatherization reduces *infiltration*. Savings scale with how leaky the home was, your climate, and your rates — not with the brand printed on the packaging.

## Diagnose before you buy a kit

Walk the house on a windy day or while HVAC is running:

1. Hold a stick of incense or a thin tissue near door edges, window locks, attic hatches, and cable penetrations.
2. Check daylight under exterior doors — a visible gap usually means the sweep or threshold needs work.
3. Inspect weatherstrip compression: flattened, cracked, or missing foam/V-strip is fair game.
4. Note *which* doors are exterior vs. garage-to-house (garage doors often need better seals than people think).
5. Photograph thresholds so you buy the right sweep style (screw-on, adhesive, door-bottom with drip cap, etc.).

If you smell gas, see scorched outlets, or suspect carbon-monoxide / combustion-air issues, stop DIY sealing around those appliances and call a qualified pro.

## Safety and building notes

> **Safety notes:** Never block required combustion air for furnaces, water heaters, or fireplaces. Do not seal dryer vents, range hoods, or bathroom fans closed. Keep bedroom egress windows operable. Use flame-resistant materials near heat sources per product labels. Ladder work for exterior casing needs fall awareness. If your home has knob-and-tube or damaged wiring in cavities you’re about to foam, consult an electrician before filling voids.

Renters: prefer removable sweeps and adhesive strips; keep original hardware to restore at move-out. Get landlord approval for screws into doors/frames when required by your lease.

## What to use where (materials cheat sheet)

| Location | Common fix | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Door bottom | Sweep, shoe, or draft “snake” | Sweeps seal better long-term; fabric snakes are temporary / rental-friendly |
| Door / window stop | Foam tape, V-strip (tension), kerf silicone bulb | Match compressed thickness to the gap |
| Sliding glass track | Pile weatherstrip / track clean + lube | Dirt often mimics a “broken seal” |
| Window sash | Rope caulk (seasonal), V-strip | Removable rope caulk is renter-friendly in winter |
| Irregular cracks | Exterior-rated caulk (not interior latex outdoors) | Prep and dry surfaces; follow cure times |
| Big holes / gaps | Backer rod + caulk, or low-expansion foam carefully | Low-expansion only near frames — foam can bow jambs |

## Install habits that keep seals working

1. Clean the surface; adhesive hates dust and silicone residue.
2. Fit dry before peeling adhesive backing.
3. Compress weatherstrip firmly without crushing it flat at rest.
4. Recheck after a week of seasonal humidity swings; retighten sweep screws.
5. Combine with the free habits in the [electric bill checklist](/blog/lower-electric-bill-priority-checklist/) — sealing a door while running A/C at 68°F with dirty filters still wastes money.

## What sealing won’t replace

- Attic insulation and air sealing at the top plates (often bigger than one door)
- Duct leaks in unconditioned spaces
- A failing [thermostat schedule](/blog/best-smart-thermostats-2026/) you never set
- Measurement when usage is still mysterious — see [Emporia Vue vs Sense](/blog/emporia-vue-vs-sense/)

## Bottom line

Start with **visible exterior-door gaps**, use a real sweep/threshold fix, then weatherstrip perimeters with materials that rebound. Treat fabric draft snakes as temporary or rental-friendly helpers.
