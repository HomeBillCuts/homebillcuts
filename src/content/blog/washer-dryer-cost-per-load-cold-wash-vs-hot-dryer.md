## Where the energy in a load goes

- **Washer motor and controls:** small — typically about **0.1–0.3 kWh per load** for a modern machine.
- **Hot water for the wash:** the big washer cost. ENERGY STAR and DOE commonly cite roughly **90% of washer energy** going to heating water. A warm or hot load can draw several gallons of hot water from your water heater.
- **Dryer:** typically the **largest single laundry cost**. Electric dryers commonly use about **2–5 kWh per load** depending on load size, spin, settings and vent condition.

## Step 1: Find your rate

Your effective electric rate = **total bill ÷ total kWh** (include delivery and fees). Gas rate = total gas bill ÷ therms. Use those, not the advertised supply rate.

## Step 2: Cost per load (kWh × rate)

| Piece of the load | Typical energy | At $0.12/kWh | At $0.17/kWh | At $0.30/kWh |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Washer motor, cold wash | ~0.2 kWh | ~$0.02 | ~$0.03 | ~$0.06 |
| Hot water, warm wash (electric WH) | ~1.5 kWh | ~$0.18 | ~$0.26 | ~$0.45 |
| Hot water, hot wash (electric WH) | ~3 kWh | ~$0.36 | ~$0.51 | ~$0.90 |
| Electric dryer, typical load | ~3.3 kWh | ~$0.40 | ~$0.56 | ~$0.99 |

These are illustrative ranges, not measurements of your machines. Gas water heating cuts the hot-water row roughly in half or more depending on gas price; a gas dryer is often about **$0.25–$0.45 per load**.

**Weekly example** (7 loads, $0.17/kWh): hot wash + electric dry ≈ 7 × ($0.03 + $0.51 + $0.56) ≈ **$7.70/week, ~$400/year**. Cold wash + electric dry ≈ **$4.10/week**. Cold wash + air-dry half the loads ≈ **$2.20/week**. That's the real-money spread.

## Step 3: Measure what you can

1. **Washer (120V):** plug it into a [Kill A Watt P4400](/blog/kill-a-watt-plug-in-energy-meter/) (check the washer's amp rating is within the meter's 15A limit), run a normal cold load, read kWh. This is motor + controls only — the hot water shows up on your water heater, not here.
2. **Electric dryer (240V):** you **can't** plug it into a Kill A Watt. Use your utility's **hourly usage graph** — run two or three loads in a quiet evening hour and compare to a normal hour — or a whole-home monitor. Nameplate watts × run time gives an upper bound.
3. **Hot water:** if you have an electric water heater, compare a day of hot-wash laundry with a cold-wash day in hourly data. The difference is the water-heating cost.

## The free moves (do these first)

- **Wash in cold** for everyday loads. Modern detergents are formulated for it.
- **Run full loads** — a half load uses much of the same energy.
- **Use the highest safe spin** — less water in the clothes means less dryer time.
- **Clean the lint filter every load** and check the vent for restriction ([vent cleaning guide](/blog/dryer-vent-cleaning-kits-energy-fire-safety/)); a clogged vent lengthens cycles and is a fire risk.
- **Use auto/moisture-sensor dry**, not long timed cycles, and dry similar fabrics together.
- **Air-dry what you can** on a [drying rack](/blog/indoor-clothes-drying-racks-cut-dryer-cost/) — the dryer is the biggest line item.
- **Water heater at ~120°F** so the hot loads you do run don't overheat water.

## Buyer decision tree

1. **Never measured anything?** A Kill A Watt for the washer and your utility's hourly data for the dryer — find your real per-load number first.
2. **Running the dryer for every load?** A drying rack is the cheapest big cut.
3. **Long dryer cycles?** Clean the vent and try wool dryer balls; time a few loads before and after.
4. **Lots of hot washes on an electric water heater?** Switch to cold first; then look at [water heater timers](/blog/electric-water-heater-timers-cut-standby/) or a [heat pump water heater](/blog/heat-pump-water-heater-vs-standard/) if it's due.
5. **Machine failing anyway?** Then compare ENERGY STAR front-loaders and heat pump dryers — don't replace a working one just for energy.

## Honest limits

- **Per-load figures are typical ranges**, not your machines; load size, spin, fabric and vent condition swing them a lot.
- **Hot water cost depends on your water heater** (electric, gas, heat pump) and its setpoint.
- **A Kill A Watt can't meter a 240V dryer.**
- **We don't guarantee any savings.**

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Compute your real electric and gas rates from the bill
- [ ] Meter one cold washer load; check dryer usage in hourly data
- [ ] Switch everyday loads to cold
- [ ] Max spin, full loads, clean lint filter + vent, sensor dry

## Bottom line

**The washer motor costs pennies; hot water and the dryer are where the money is.** Wash cold, spin hard, keep the vent clean, air-dry what you can — that can roughly halve a typical household's laundry cost.
