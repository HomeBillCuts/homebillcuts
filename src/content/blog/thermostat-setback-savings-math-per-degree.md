## Why setbacks work (the physics in one line)

Heat loss is roughly proportional to **indoor temperature minus outdoor temperature**. Lower the indoor number and the house leaks less heat for every hour it sits cooler. The morning recovery doesn't "pay back" what you saved — for furnaces and resistance heat you come out ahead. (Heat pumps: see the exception below.)

## Step 1: Find your heating dollars per month

- **Gas heat:** winter gas bill − summer gas bill (water heater/stove baseline) = heating dollars.
- **Electric heat:** winter kWh − shoulder-month kWh (April/October) = heating kWh. **Heating kWh × your rate ($/kWh)** = heating dollars. Your rate = total bill ÷ total kWh.

Example: January electric 2,100 kWh, October 800 kWh → 1,300 heating kWh × $0.17 = **$221/month of heat**.

## Step 2: Apply the honest rule of thumb

DOE's guidance (7–10°F for 8 hours/day → up to ~10%/year) works out to **roughly 1% of heating cost per degree, per 8-hour daily setback**. Treat it as a starting estimate.

| Monthly heating cost | 4°F night setback | 8°F night setback | 8°F night + 8°F workday away |
|---|---|---|---|
| $100 | ~$4 | ~$8 | ~$16 |
| $200 | ~$8 | ~$16 | ~$32 |
| $300 | ~$12 | ~$24 | ~$48 |

Over a 5-month heating season, an 8°F night setback on a $200/month heating bill is roughly **$80** — real money, but not "cut your bill in half."

Savings are **bigger** in leaky houses and mild climates (bigger share of time the setback actually holds) and **smaller** in tight, well-insulated homes that barely cool down overnight.

## Step 3: Measure, don't trust

Your whole-house heat can't be plugged into a Kill A Watt — but you can still test:

1. **Utility interval data:** most electric utilities show daily or hourly kWh online. Run two weeks without setbacks, two weeks with, and compare kWh per **heating degree day** (from your utility or weather sites) so a cold snap doesn't fool you.
2. **Gas meter reads:** read the meter at the same time each morning for the same A/B weeks.
3. **Space-heater households:** if you zone-heat, put the heater on a [Kill A Watt](/blog/kill-a-watt-plug-in-energy-meter/) and log its weekly kWh × rate alongside the central-heat change, so you know the setback isn't just shifting cost into the space heater.

## The exceptions

- **Heat pumps:** big setbacks can trigger **auxiliary resistance strips** (often 5–15 kW) during recovery. Use 2–4°F setbacks, enable smart/adaptive recovery, and check aux runtime. See [heat pump aux heat settings](/blog/heat-pump-aux-heat-high-bill-thermostat-settings/).
- **Radiant floors and boilers with slow response:** long recovery times; use smaller setbacks or longer pre-heat windows.
- **Time-of-use rates:** pre-heat before peak, then coast through it.
- **Health and pipes:** keep vulnerable occupants warm; never go below ~55°F away in freezing weather.

## Free first

- Program the thermostat you already have (most programmables have night/away schedules nobody set).
- Lower the "home" setpoint 1–2°F and add a sweater; that's often worth more than any setback.
- Fix the [biggest drafts](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/) and [filters](/blog/hvac-air-filters-merv-efficiency/) so the setback holds.

## Buyer decision tree

1. **Manual dial thermostat, regular schedule?** A programmable or basic smart thermostat pays off fastest.
2. **Already programmable and you keep the schedule?** Don't buy — savings from upgrading are small.
3. **Irregular schedule / forget to set back?** Smart with geofencing (Ecobee, Nest, Amazon).
4. **Uneven rooms?** Models with remote room sensors (Ecobee, Honeywell T9).
5. **Heat pump?** Pick one with heat-pump/aux-aware logic and confirm wiring compatibility first.
6. **Electric baseboard?** Standard low-voltage smart thermostats won't work — see [line-voltage thermostats](/blog/electric-baseboard-heater-thermostats-line-voltage/).

## Honest limits

- **The ~1%/degree figure is a rule of thumb**, not a measurement of your home.
- **Weather swamps small changes** — normalize by heating degree days.
- **Heat pump savings depend on aux behavior.**
- **We don't guarantee any savings.**
