If cold air spills out of your fireplace on a windy winter night, you are not imagining it. Warm indoor air rises, finds an unused flue, and dumps out the chimney like a vertical hole open to outdoors. A leaky or missing damper makes that path worse. The fix for an idle fireplace is usually a removable draft stopper—a magnetic cover across the opening, a wool or felt plug in the flue, or an inflatable chimney balloon—never something you leave in place for a live fire.

## The short version

- Close the damper fully first. If it seats tightly and you feel no hearth draft, you may not need a product.
- Magnetic cover for an iron or steel surround: fast, visible, and hard to forget before someone lights a fire.
- Wool or felt flue plug, or a chimney balloon, when you need a seal *inside* the flue above a broken damper—and you have measured that opening.
- Never block a flue you still use. Remove every cover, plug, and balloon before any flame. Never use an in-flue blocker with operable vented gas logs still connected.
- Damper repair or a pro top-sealing damper beats stacking seasonal products if you will burn again next year.

## Why the hearth feels cold

Warm air rises. A chimney is a tall, cold shaft open (or poorly closed) to outdoors, so stack effect pulls conditioned air up the flue and replaces it with outdoor air through every other leak in the house. Even with the damper “closed,” many older dampers warp, do not seat, or leave large gaps. An open damper on a fireplace you never use is a continuous exhaust you did not ask for.

A blocker helps most when you feel cold air or chimney odor at the hearth with the damper shut, the fireplace sits unused all winter, the damper is missing or will not latch, and the opening faces a room you use every evening.

It helps less when the damper already seals, the real problem is an empty attic or major duct leaks, you only drape a loose panel over the face while leaving the damper wide open, you still need the fireplace for heat this season, or the chimney has structural damage, heavy creosote, or animal nests that need a sweep or mason first.

A sealed idle flue can cut a noticeable draft and ease HVAC runtime in leaky older homes—especially on cold, windy nights. Treat the downdraft as a reason to inspect and choose the right removable seal, not a promise for your address.

**Rule of thumb:** magnetic cover for a ferrous surround face seal; wool plug or balloon for a seal in the flue above a broken damper. Fix the damper (or hire a top-sealing damper) if you will burn regularly.

## What to do first

1. Confirm the fireplace will stay unused for the season (or permanently). Blockers are for idle openings only.
2. Open and close the damper fully. If it seats and you feel no draft on a cold windy day, stop shopping. If it will not close, will not latch, or daylight shows through gaps, plan a removable blocker—and eventually a damper repair.
3. Magnet-test the surround. Hold a fridge magnet where the cover would cling. Continuous iron or steel → magnetic cover is on the table. Bare brick, stone, tile, wood mantel, or non-magnetic stainless → skip magnets; plan a flue-level wool plug or balloon.
4. Measure the opening for magnetic covers (width × height of the face or metal frame). Common retail size is about 39″ × 32″—verify magnets will reach continuous metal, not only thin trim.
5. Measure the flue for plugs and balloons (inside dimensions where the product will sit, often 1–2 feet above the firebox). Length × depth for rectangular flues; diameter for round. Match the manufacturer’s fit range—balloons usually want an opening slightly smaller than the labeled maximum.
6. Photograph the opening and damper hardware so you do not order a plug that cannot clear the damper flap or smoke shelf.
7. Plan the reminder tag. Hang it where everyone sees it so nobody lights a fire with the blocker still in.

## What not to do

> Read this before installing anything in or over a fireplace.

- **Never with an active fire.** Remove the cover, wool plug, or balloon completely before lighting wood, pellets, or any flame.
- **Never with operable vented gas logs still connected.** Disable or remove vented gas logs before any in-flue plug or balloon. Unvented (“vent-free”) gas appliances have their own code and CO rules—do not DIY-block those without a qualified tech.
- **Keep carbon monoxide alarms working** on every floor. If you smell combustion odors, get pilot outages, or CO alarms sound after sealing, remove the blocker and call a pro.
- **Magnets only on ferrous frames.** Bare masonry and many stainless surrounds will not hold a magnetic cover.
- **Measure the flue** for in-flue products. A too-small balloon can fall; a forced oversized plug can damage tile.
- **Remove for chimney sweeps and inspections.** Tell your sweep the product is there.
- **Do not plug over structural damage, heavy creosote, or nests.** A draft stopper is not a chimney repair.
- **Do not seal a chimney that still vents an appliance** without a qualified tech confirming the path and code.

If you smell gas, see scorched dampers, or suspect CO or combustion-air problems, stop and call a pro.

## Which one should you buy?

- **You will burn this fireplace during heating season?** Skip the seasonal product. Fix or replace the damper.
- **Operable vented gas logs still connected?** Do not install an in-flue plug or balloon until a tech disables or removes them.
- **Damper already seats tightly and no hearth draft?** Put the budget into doors, attic stairs, or attic depth instead ([checklist](/blog/lower-electric-bill-priority-checklist/), [weatherstripping](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/), [attic stair covers](/blog/attic-stair-hatch-insulation-covers/)).
- **Iron or steel surround that holds a magnet across the frame?** Magnetic fireplace cover sized to the opening (often ~39×32—verify your width and height).
- **Bare masonry, tile, stone, or non-magnetic stainless?** Skip magnets. Use a wool plug or chimney balloon sized to the flue.
- **You can reach the flue and want puncture-proof?** Wool or felt flue plug matched to the measured flue (for example a ~12×12 class when that matches).
- **Measured rectangular or square flue in a balloon fit range?** Inflatable chimney balloon matched to the listing min/max. Inflate until snug; keep the reminder handle visible.
- **You will burn again next season and the damper is the real failure?** Removable blocker as a winter bridge only; plan a pro damper or top-seal.
- **Empty attic or attic stairs dump more heat than this flue?** Block the fireplace *and* prioritize [attic stair covers](/blog/attic-stair-hatch-insulation-covers/), [attic insulation](/blog/attic-insulation-approaches-when-to-call-pro/), and [air sealing](/blog/air-sealing-caulk-foam-outlet-gaskets/).

## Install habits that keep blockers working

1. Idle confirm first—leave the reminder tag where everyone sees it.
2. Magnetic covers—wipe the frame; seat on continuous ferrous metal; feel the perimeter for gaps.
3. Wool plugs—seat at the measured flue tile above the damper when present; do not force an oversized pad.
4. Chimney balloons—inflate until snug; keep the retrieval handle and reminder accessible from the firebox.
5. Never “test” with a fire—fully remove the blocker before any flame, every time.
6. Tell your chimney sweep and pull the product before brush or camera work.
7. Revisit after the first cold windy week—feel for hearth drafts; reseat magnets or re-inflate before assuming failure.

## What a fireplace blocker will not replace

- A working damper if you burn regularly—seasonal seals are for unused openings.
- Chimney structural, creosote, or animal problems—need a sweep or mason.
- Other stack-effect holes—[attic stair covers](/blog/attic-stair-hatch-insulation-covers/), [attic insulation](/blog/attic-insulation-approaches-when-to-call-pro/), [air sealing](/blog/air-sealing-caulk-foam-outlet-gaskets/).

## Longer-term fixes when you will burn again

Seasonal blockers are for idle fireplaces. If you want fires next winter, plan a real damper solution:

- **Damper repair or replacement** when the existing throat damper warps, sticks, or will not seat. A chimney pro can tell you whether the hardware is salvageable.
- **Top-sealing (chimney-top) damper** mounted at the crown, often with a cable you pull from the firebox. These usually seal better than old throat dampers because they close at the top of the flue instead of halfway down a warped throat. Installation is a roof job—budget for a pro.
- **Glass fireplace doors** that latch can cut room air exchange at the opening, but they do not replace a working damper, and poorly fitted doors still leak. They are a comfort upgrade, not a substitute for flue sealing when the damper is gone.
- **Chimney cap** keeps rain, animals, and some downdrafts out. It does not replace a closed damper or a plug when the flue is otherwise open to the house.

Use a magnetic cover, wool plug, or balloon as a winter bridge on an unused opening. Schedule damper work before the next burn season rather than lighting fires around a forgotten plug.

## Bottom line

Treat an unused fireplace as a hole to outdoors. Close and check the damper, magnet-test the surround, measure the opening or flue, then pick a magnetic cover, wool plug, chimney balloon, or a real damper repair. Follow the safety list—especially remove-before-fire and never with operable vented gas logs—install for a continuous removable seal, and keep going with weatherstripping and attic work rather than stopping at one product.
