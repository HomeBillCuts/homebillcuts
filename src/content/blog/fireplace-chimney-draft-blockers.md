## Why unused fireplaces leak heat

Warm indoor air rises. A chimney is a tall, cold shaft open (or poorly closed) to outdoors, so **stack effect** pulls conditioned air up the flue and replaces it with cold outdoor air through every other leak in the house. Even with a damper “closed,” many older dampers warp, don’t seat, or leave large gaps. An open damper on a fireplace you never use is one of the largest single holes in a typical living room envelope.

**Honest framing:** A sealed flue can cut a noticeable draft and reduce HVAC runtime in a leaky older home — especially on windy cold nights. Savings scale with how open the damper was, flue size, climate, fuel and rates, and whether you also sealed other paths. We do **not** guarantee a dollar cut. Treat a cold downdraft at the hearth as motivation to inspect the damper and choose a removable blocker — not as a promise for your address.

## Product types at a glance

| Type | Best when | Limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Magnetic fireplace cover** (fabric/panel with magnets) | Iron or steel fireplace surround / frame; want a room-facing seal without climbing into the flue | Magnets need **ferrous** metal — won’t stick to bare brick, stone, tile, or non-magnetic stainless; seals the opening face, not deep in the flue |
| **Wool / felt flue plug** (pressure-fit pad) | You can reach the flue tile above the firebox; want a durable, puncture-proof plug | Must match flue size; trim carefully; remove before any fire or sweep |
| **Inflatable chimney balloon** (Chimney Balloon / pillow) | Measured rectangular or square flue; damper missing or leaky; seasonal seal | Must inflate to fit; can deflate if punctured; size-critical; never with active vented gas logs |

**Rule of thumb:** magnetic cover for a quick living-room face seal on an iron insert surround; wool plug or balloon when you need a seal *in* the flue above a broken damper.

## Measure & fit before you buy

1. **Confirm the fireplace is truly idle** for the season (or permanently) — blockers are for *unused* openings.
2. **Check the damper** — if it closes tightly and you feel no draft, you may not need a product yet. If it won’t close or daylight shows, plan a removable blocker *and* eventually a damper repair.
3. **Magnetic covers:** Measure the **opening width × height** of the fireplace face (or the metal frame you want the magnets to grip). Common retail size is about **39″ × 32″** — verify your frame is iron/steel and that magnets will reach continuous metal, not only thin trim.
4. **Flue plugs and balloons:** Measure the **inside of the flue** where the product will sit (often 1–2 feet above the firebox, above the damper if present). Length × depth for rectangular flues; diameter for round. Order a size that matches the manufacturer’s fit range — balloons usually want an opening slightly smaller than the labeled max.
5. Photograph the opening and any damper hardware so you don’t order a plug that can’t clear the damper flap.

When in doubt, measure twice — returns on sooty flue products are miserable.

## Safety first (non-negotiable)

> **Read this before installing anything in or over a fireplace.**

- **Never with an active fire.** Remove the cover, wool plug, or balloon completely before lighting wood, pellets, or any flame. Leave a bright reminder tag hanging in the opening if the product includes one.
- **Remove before burning** every time — no exceptions for “just a small fire.”
- **Never with an active vented gas log set.** Inflatable balloons and flue plugs must **not** be used with vented gas logs that are still connected and operable. For safety, a vented gas log set must be **disabled or removed** before using a flue plug or chimney balloon. Unvented (“vent-free”) gas appliances have their own code and CO rules — do not DIY-block those openings without a qualified tech.
- **Carbon monoxide and combustion air:** Fireplaces, furnaces, and water heaters that need draft or make-up air can be affected by aggressive sealing elsewhere in the house. If you smell combustion odors, get pilot outages, or CO alarms sound after sealing, remove the blocker and call a pro. Maintain working **CO alarms** on every floor.
- **Magnets only on ferrous / iron frames.** Magnetic covers need iron or steel to cling. Bare brick, stone, tile, wood mantels, and many stainless surrounds will not hold magnets safely — use a flue-level plug or balloon instead (or a non-magnetic panel method rated for the opening).
- **Measure the flue** for in-flue products; a too-small balloon or pad can fall or leak; a forced oversized plug can damage tile.
- **Remove for chimney sweeps and inspections.** Tell your sweep the product is there; pull it before any brush or camera work.
- **Kids and pets:** Magnets and hanging reminder strings are curiosities — install so they can’t be pulled into the firebox play zone.

If the chimney has structural damage, heavy creosote, or animal nests, fix those first — a draft blocker is not a repair.

## When a draft blocker helps *your* bill

It can help when:

- You feel cold air at the hearth or smell chimney odors with the damper “closed.”
- The fireplace sits unused all winter while the furnace or heat pump runs hard.
- The damper is missing, warped, or won’t latch.
- You’ve already done free wins on the [checklist](/blog/lower-electric-bill-priority-checklist/) and obvious [door weatherstripping](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/).

It usually won’t move the needle much when:

- The damper already seals tightly and there’s no detectable draft.
- The house’s real problem is an empty attic, major duct leaks, or failing HVAC.
- You only cover the opening cosmetically while leaving a wide-open damper behind a loose panel.
- You need the fireplace for heat this season — blockers are for *idle* flues.

## Bottom line

For an **unused** fireplace, stop the stack-effect leak with a product that matches the opening: **magnetic cover** on ferrous frames, **wool flue plug** or **chimney balloon** sized to the flue. Prioritize safety — never with an active fire, remove before burning, never with active vented gas logs unless disabled/removed, mind CO and combustion air, and pull the blocker for sweeps.
