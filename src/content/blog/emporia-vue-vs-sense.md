Whole-home energy monitors promise to show *what* is driving your electric bill. Two names come up constantly: **Emporia Vue** and **Sense**. This comparison is for US homeowners who want bill-relevant visibility — not a gadget for gadget’s sake — and who already know that monitors don’t save kWh by themselves.

Behavior change and repairs do. If you need the action order first, read the [priority checklist](/blog/lower-electric-bill-priority-checklist/) and [Start Here](/start-here/).

## Who should buy a home energy monitor?

Good fit:

- You have unexplained usage spikes and utility interval data isn’t enough.
- You’re hunting a bad well pump, heat strip, pool pump schedule, or failing fridge.
- You want circuit-level trends before upgrading HVAC or panels.
- You’re willing to look at the data weekly for at least a month.

Poor fit:

- You haven’t replaced filters, fixed setpoints, or read your bill yet.
- You want guaranteed savings printed on the box.
- Nobody in the home will open the app.
- You’re unwilling to hire an electrician for a panel install.

## How each approach helps your bill

### Emporia Vue: explicit circuits

Vue-style systems shine when you care about **known big loads**: air handler, EVSE, dryer, water heater, kitchen receptacles, etc. You clamp the mains and (in expanded kits) individual breakers, then read real circuit energy.

**Strengths**

- Clear kWh by circuit when sensors are installed correctly
- Helpful for TOU planning and verifying heat-pump aux strips
- Often expandable as you label more breakers
- Solar production monitoring options on supported kits

**Tradeoffs**

- You must map and label circuits (time investment)
- Panel space / knockout / wire-management constraints are real
- More sensors → more install complexity and cost
- App polish varies; focus is data over magic inferences

### Sense: detection-first

Sense focuses on recognizing device signatures from the main service clamps. Over time it may identify the dishwasher, dryer, or other loads without a clamp on every breaker.

**Strengths**

- Faster “what just turned on?” insight after detections mature
- Cleaner install in many panels (fewer CTs)
- Strong storytelling in the app for household engagement
- Useful when circuit access is messy but mains are reachable

**Tradeoffs**

- Detections are probabilistic — verify before blaming a device
- Similar loads can confuse classification
- Less precise than a dedicated clamp when you need accounting-grade circuit totals
- Some advanced features / history may sit behind account tiers — check current pricing

## Install and safety (both products)

> **Safety note (non-negotiable):** Working inside an electrical panel can kill or cause fire. If you are not a qualified electrician comfortable with live/load-side work, **hire one**. De-energize when required, use proper PPE, respect utility seals, and follow the manufacturer’s instructions and local code. HomeBillCuts does not provide electrical contracting advice.

Practical install tips:

- Photograph the panel schedule and wiring before starting.
- Confirm amperage rating of service and choose correctly sized CTs.
- Mind neutral/ground rules and manufacturer torque guidance.
- Leave space for future breakers; don’t create spaghetti that blocks the deadfront.
- After power-up, compare monitor totals to the utility meter over 24–72 hours as a sanity check.

## Cost mindset (no fake prices)

Instead of invented street prices, budget for:

1. Hardware kit (mains-only vs. multi-circuit)
2. Electrician labor if you’re not DIY-qualified (often the larger line item)
3. Optional sensors / solar CTs
4. Your time to label circuits or verify detections

A monitor that sits unused is the most expensive option.

## Which should you buy?

**Choose Emporia Vue-style monitoring if:**

- You want breaker-level accountability
- You’re optimizing EV charging, HVAC stages, or a shop subpanel
- You’re comfortable mapping circuits (or paying an electrician to)
- You prefer measured clamps over inferred devices

**Choose Sense if:**

- You want quicker household-friendly insights from mains clamps
- Your goal is finding mystery loads without cluttering every breaker
- You’re okay verifying detections and living with some ambiguity
- Panel space for many CTs is limited

**Consider neither yet if:**

- You haven’t finished the [priority checklist](/blog/lower-electric-bill-priority-checklist/)
- Your utility already provides granular interval data you’re ignoring
- A $30 plug-in meter would answer the only question you have (one appliance)

## A practical evaluation week

1. Install (or have installed) and verify totals vs. the utility meter.
2. Label the top 5 expected loads.
3. Watch one hot afternoon or cold morning — note HVAC staging behavior.
4. Pick **one** wasteful pattern to change (schedule, setpoint, or repair).
5. Recheck the following week’s kWh. Keep or return based on whether you acted — not based on charts alone.

## Bottom line

**Emporia Vue** wins when you want clamp-accurate circuit stories. **Sense** wins when you want detection-driven mystery hunting with a lighter sensor footprint. Neither replaces weatherization, maintenance, or sane thermostat habits — and neither guarantees a lower bill.
