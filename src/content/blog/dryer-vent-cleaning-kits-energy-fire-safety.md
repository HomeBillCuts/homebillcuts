Clothes that need **two or three cycles** to dry are often a vent problem, not a “weak dryer.” A lint-packed or crushed dryer duct makes the machine run longer — more **kWh** on electric dryers, more **therms** on gas — and raises **lint fire** risk. ## When vent cleaning actually moves your bill

Cleaning (or replacing) the dryer vent helps most when:

- Loads that used to finish in one cycle now need **extra time or a second run**.
- The dryer or laundry closet feels **much hotter** than it used to, or the exterior hood barely exhales warm air.
- You see **lint buildup** at the outdoor hood, crushed foil duct behind the dryer, or a long flexible run with many tight bends.
- The machine is **electric resistance** (high watts for a long time) or **gas** (longer burner runtime + lint near a heat source).

It helps less — or isn’t the first lever — when:

- You’re overloading the drum, using ultra-heavy bedding without splitting loads, or skipping the **lint screen** every cycle.
- The dryer is near end-of-life (failed heating element, bad thermistor, dying drum rollers) — a clean vent won’t resurrect dead parts.
- Outdoor temperature and humidity are extreme and you’re judging one rainy week — look at a pattern, not one load.

**Honest framing:** A clear vent restores **designed airflow**. That can shorten runtime and cut energy use *toward* what the dryer was meant to use — it does **not** turn a 30-amp electric dryer into a thrifty appliance, and we do **not** guarantee a dollar cut on your next bill. Fire-risk reduction is a separate, serious reason to keep the path clear.

## Quick diagnosis (5–10 minutes)

1. **Lint screen every load** — if it’s caked after one cycle, clean it; clogged screens alone stretch dry times.
2. **Exterior hood test** — with the dryer on high heat / timed dry, the outdoor flap should open and push strong warm air. Weak puff, cold air, or a flap stuck shut → restrict downstream.
3. **Look behind the dryer** — white vinyl / thin foil accordion duct that is crushed, kinked, or longer than needed is a common DIY failure. Prefer short, smooth metal transition duct.
4. **Smell / heat** — burning lint smell, scorching-hot transition duct, or breaker trips under load → stop and investigate before the next load.
5. **Runtime log** — note minutes for a normal towel load before and after cleaning. Your bill’s **kWh** (and a [monitor](/blog/emporia-vue-vs-sense/) if you have one) is the honest scoreboard.

## DIY brush kits vs hiring a pro

| Situation | Lean DIY | Call a pro |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short, accessible run; outdoor hood reachable | Brush kit + vacuum attachment | Optional |
| Long concealed runs through floors/attics | Maybe brush from both ends | Often better |
| Roof termination, multi-story chase, or masonry | Usually not | Yes |
| Suspected pest nests, disconnected duct, or water intrusion | Inspect only | Yes |
| Gas dryer + CO concerns / irregular flame behavior | Stop DIY experiments | Qualified tech |

Renters: get landlord approval before disconnecting gas lines or cutting holes. Unplug electric dryers before pulling them out; for gas, know how to shut the valve and check for leaks — or hire it.

## Safety non-negotiables

> **Lint in dryer ducts is a leading cause of dryer fires. Treat airflow and clearances as safety work, not just a bill hack.**

- **Unplug** electric dryers (or shut off the correct breaker) before disconnecting the duct. For **gas** dryers, shut the gas valve and follow the manual — if you’re not comfortable, hire a pro.
- Never vent a dryer into a **garage, crawlspace, attic, or chimney**. Exhaust must terminate outdoors with a proper hood.
- Do **not** use plastic / vinyl flexible duct or thin foil that crushes easily for the dryer transition — many manufacturers and codes expect **UL 2158A**-listed transition duct (semi-rigid or rigid metal). Replace junk duct; don’t just brush it and shove it back crushed.
- Keep the outdoor hood clear of **lint, snow, bird screens clogged with lint, and landscaping**.
- After cleaning, run an empty timed cycle and re-check the exterior airflow before loading a full week of laundry.
- Working **smoke alarms** on every level still matter. Gas appliances elsewhere still need **CO alarms**.

If you smell gas, see scorch marks, or the dryer repeatedly overheats — stop using it and get it serviced.

## Foil accordion vs metal duct (quick tradeoffs)

| Type | Role | Watch-outs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Thin foil / vinyl flex** | Often stock behind moved dryers | Crushes, traps lint, tears — replace for the dryer transition |
| **UL 2158A semi-rigid aluminum** | Proper short transition dryer → wall | Cut to length; secure with metal worm-gear clamps; don’t leave excess coiled |
| **Rigid metal (pro runs)** | Long concealed paths | DIY only if you’re equipped; many homes need a vent pro |
| **Brush / vacuum kits** | Clean lint from existing paths | Won’t fix a disconnected or crushed duct by themselves |

**Rule of thumb:** clean what’s clogged; **replace** what’s crushed or non-listed. Brushing a pancake-flat foil hose is theater.

## How to use a brush kit without making a mess

1. Pull the dryer straight out carefully (protect flooring). Disconnect the transition duct.
2. Vacuum the dryer outlet stub and the lint screen housing per the manual.
3. From the **outdoor hood** and/or dryer end, run the brush **with the drill on low**, turning in the direction the kit instructs (usually clockwise) so rods don’t unscrew inside the duct.
4. Work in sections on long runs; don’t force a 90° elbow — slow down, then continue.
5. Vacuum debris at both ends. Wipe the outdoor flap so it swings freely.
6. Reinstall with **short, smooth metal duct**, two snug **metal clamps**, and no screws that protrude into the airstream (lint catchers).
7. Test airflow outdoors on a timed cycle.

## Aftercare (keep the win)

1. Clean the **lint filter every load** — no exceptions.
2. Every few months, check the **outdoor hood** for lint and a free-moving flap.
3. Pull the dryer once or twice a year (more if you have pets) and vacuum the transition stub.
4. Keep loads reasonable; heavy comforters often need **split cycles** even with a clear vent.
5. Pair laundry timing with your rate plan on the [priority checklist](/blog/lower-electric-bill-priority-checklist/) — a clear vent plus off-peak drying stacks the easy wins.

## Bottom line

Dryer vent cleaning is one of the few “maintenance” tasks that can cut **runtime energy** and **fire risk** at the same time. Brush out lint, replace crushed non-listed foil with proper metal transition duct, verify strong airflow at the outdoor hood, and re-check cycle times. Skip guaranteed-savings claims on kit listings — your duct length, dryer type, and habits decide the outcome.
