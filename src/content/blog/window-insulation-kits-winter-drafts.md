Single-pane and leaky double-hung windows dump heat all winter. Indoor **shrink-film window insulation kits** are a cheap, removable way to cut drafts before you jump to full window replacement. This guide is **bill-first**: walk the house before you buy a carton, know which rooms to film first (and which to skip), install so the tape holds through the first cold night, and pick the right pack size — without inventing dollar savings.

Prefer the ranked path? Start with the [priority checklist](/blog/lower-electric-bill-priority-checklist/) and [Start Here](/start-here/). Edge seals (sweeps, V-strip) belong in the [weatherstripping guide](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/); daily-use [thermal / blackout curtains](/blog/thermal-blackout-curtains-energy-savings/) are a separate layer. Frame and outlet leaks film won’t fix live in [air sealing](/blog/air-sealing-caulk-foam-outlet-gaskets/). A sealed room also makes [oil-filled zone heating](/blog/oil-filled-space-heaters-zone-heating/) less wasteful. This page is about **film across the glass face**.

## When shrink film helps *your* bill

It can help when:

- You feel cold air at the glass or sash, or rooms with big window walls never hold setpoint.
- Windows are **single-pane**, storm-window missing, or older double-hung with tired sash seals.
- You already fixed setpoints and dirty [HVAC filters](/blog/hvac-air-filters-merv-efficiency/) — infiltration is the next cheap lever.
- You rent or you’re waiting on a real window project and need a **seasonal** fix.

It usually won’t help much when:

- Units are already good sealed double- or triple-pane with intact weatherstrip and no sash play.
- The real driver is failed HVAC, electric strip heat thrashing, or a wide-open attic (see [attic insulation](/blog/attic-insulation-approaches-when-to-call-pro/)).
- You leave operable **egress** bedrooms sealed shut (unsafe) or block combustion-appliance air paths.

**Honest framing:** Film reduces *infiltration and some conductive loss* by creating a still air gap. Savings scale with how leaky the window was, your climate, and your rates — not with the brand on the box. We do **not** guarantee a dollar cut. Treat film as a reversible envelope step, not a substitute for attic air sealing or a failed heating system.

## Measure first: walk the house before you buy a carton

A 9-pack before a 15-minute walk is how you end up with leftover sheets and tape that won’t stick to chalky trim. Do this once:

1. **Feel for drafts on a windy day or after sunset** — walk each exterior window with the back of your hand (or incense). Note cold glass vs air moving at the sash/stop.
2. **List the worst rooms first** — living-room picture walls, glass patio doors, bedrooms that never warm up, basement hoppers that whistle. Skip rooms that already feel fine.
3. **Measure the frame perimeter where tape will land**, not just the glass daylight. Kit sizes (e.g. 42″ × 62″) are *sheet* sizes — compare to the painted/vinyl landing zone around the opening.
4. **Note trim type** — smooth vinyl, painted wood, chalky latex, or silicone caulk residue. Dirty or silicone-contaminated landings are why “the kit failed,” not the plastic itself.
5. **Count openings by size class** — standard double-hung vs oversized patio/picture glass. Don’t buy nine patio sheets for six bedroom windows.
6. **Check egress and combustion air** before you stretch anything — see the do-not-film list below. Safety beats a cozy draft fix.

If you still can’t tell whether glass or a door gap is the villain, seal the door first with a sweep from the [weatherstripping guide](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/) and re-check — film on glass won’t fix a banana-shaped exterior door.

## Room-by-room: what to film first (and what to skip)

### Prioritize (biggest comfort + bill leverage)

1. **Living / family rooms with cold glass walls** — long evening hours and big surface area.
2. **Patio / sliding glass doors** — often the single worst winter surface. Measure carefully; use the oversized sheet when standard kits don’t cover the frame landing.
3. **Primary bedrooms that fight the thermostat overnight** — film can help *if* you keep a safe egress path (see below). Pair with [thermal curtains](/blog/thermal-blackout-curtains-energy-savings/) for nighttime layering.
4. **Home office / daytime occupied rooms** with single-pane or storm-less glass — hours of occupancy beat perfecting a guest closet.
5. **Basement hopper / ground-level windows** that whistle — leftover scraps often work; still respect egress and dry wet masonry first.

### Do-not-film / do-not-rush list

- **Egress bedrooms while occupied for sleep** — do not permanently film over the only operable exit. Prefer curtains + weatherstrip, or leave a clear peel-in-seconds path.
- **Combustion air paths** — never seal dryer vents, range hoods, bathroom fans, furnace/water-heater combustion-air intakes, or fireplace make-up air with film or tape.
- **Good sealed double- or triple-pane units** with intact weatherstrip and no sash play — you’ll spend more time than you save.
- **Windows you open daily** for pets, smokers, or ventilation — film is seasonal and fragile.
- **Chalky, wet, or silicone-slick trim you haven’t cleaned** — fix prep first or tape lifts on night one.
- **Spaces where a space heater sits inches from the glass** — keep film away from heater surfaces and open flames; zone heat belongs on purpose-built gear like [oil-filled heaters](/blog/oil-filled-space-heaters-zone-heating/).

Film the **worst two rooms** first, live with them through a cold snap, then decide if the rest of the house is worth another pack.

| Job | Better tool |
| --- | --- |
| Gap under an exterior door | Door sweep / threshold ([weatherstripping](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/)) |
| Flat sash / stop leaks you can feel with incense | V-strip, foam tape, rope caulk |
| Baseboards, penetrations, exterior-wall outlets | [Caulk, foam & outlet gaskets](/blog/air-sealing-caulk-foam-outlet-gaskets/) |
| Whole window face drafts / “cold glass” rooms | Indoor shrink film kit (this page) |
| Daily open/close + light control on cold glass | [Thermal / blackout curtains](/blog/thermal-blackout-curtains-energy-savings/) |
| Failed, warped exterior door slab | Insulated exterior door (measure first) |
| Rotten sash, fogged IGUs, structural failure | Pro window replacement — film is a bridge, not a remodel |

Film does **not** replace fixing a banana-shaped door or a wide-open attic. Pair film on glass with sweeps on doors when both leak. If the attic hatch dumps conditioned air, fix that path before you obsess over every guest-room pane — see [attic insulation](/blog/attic-insulation-approaches-when-to-call-pro/).

## Buyer decision tree (pick the right pack)

Use this before you cart a whole-house carton:

1. **Learning the technique on 1–3 standard windows?** → **3-pack** (42″ × 62″). Confirm tape sticks on *your* trim before you buy nine.
2. **Three isn’t enough, nine feels like overkill?** → **4-pack** same sheet size.
3. **Tape already holds and you have many same-size openings?** → **9-pack** for better unit cost.
4. **Patio door or oversized picture glass beyond 42×62 frame coverage?** → **Patio / oversized sheet** (84″ × 110″ class). Measure twice; enlist a second person.
5. **Tempted by outdoor exterior film?** → Different product and install. This page is **indoor** shrink kits only.
6. **Still undecided?** → Door/sash edge leak → [weatherstripping](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/) first. Need daytime open/close → [thermal curtains](/blog/thermal-blackout-curtains-energy-savings/). Fogged/rotted glass → contractor quote; film is a seasonal bridge.

**Measure tip:** Kit dimensions are *sheet* sizes, not glass daylight. Compare to the **frame perimeter** where tape will land.

## Safety and renter notes

> **Safety:** Keep bedroom egress windows operable when occupied for sleeping — do not permanently film over the only exit. Never seal dryer vents, range hoods, or bathroom fans closed. Do not block required combustion air for furnaces, water heaters, or fireplaces. Use the hair dryer carefully; keep film away from space-heater surfaces and open flames.

Renters: film + kit tape is usually removable in spring; test a small frame area first. Keep original hardware and get landlord approval if your lease restricts adhesives on painted trim.

## Install so the tape actually holds

1. **Clean and dry** the frame where tape lands — dust, chalk, and silicone residue are why kits “fail.” Isopropyl alcohol on a cloth helps on painted/varnished wood and vinyl; let it fully dry.
2. Apply double-sided tape firmly; press the full length (not just corners). Many installs improve if you wait **10–15 minutes** after peeling the backing before stretching film.
3. Cut film oversized; attach to tape without wrinkles at the perimeter; leave a little give if the wall flexes in wind.
4. Shrink with a hair dryer on medium heat, keep moving, and stop when the film is taut — overheating melts holes.
5. Trim excess with a sharp blade *outside* the sealed area.
6. Recheck after the first cold windy night; reinforce weak corners with clear packing tape if needed.

Basement hoppers and window-AC side gaps can use leftover scraps — still keep required ventilation and egress in mind. Pull window-AC sleeves snug *before* you film around them, or you’ll trap a leaky path behind pretty plastic.

### Troubleshooting: tape failure, vinyl vs paint, first cold night

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tape lifts in corners overnight | Dust, chalk, or damp landing | Peel, clean with isopropyl, dry fully, re-tape |
| Tape won’t stick to vinyl | Oils / silicone residue | Wipe with isopropyl; avoid oily protectant sprays on the landing |
| Tape pulls paint off wood | Weak or glossy latex | Test a small area; film the stop instead of fragile casings — or use curtains |
| Hole melted during shrink | Dryer held still / heat too high | Patch with clear packing tape, or cut a new sheet; keep the dryer moving |
| Film pops on first cold night | Thermal contraction + weak corner | Reinforce corners; leave a touch more slack on long patio spans |
| Condensation between film and glass | Humidity + cold glass (common) | Wipe when you can; film still cuts drafts — wipe isn’t failure |

If tape fails repeatedly on the same opening, fix prep — or switch that opening to weatherstrip + [thermal curtains](/blog/thermal-blackout-curtains-energy-savings/) instead of fighting chalky trim all winter.

## FAQ

### Do indoor shrink-film window kits lower heating bills?

They can help on single-pane or leaky windows by cutting drafts and creating a still air gap. Savings depend on how leaky the window was, your climate, and your rates. Film is a seasonal fix—not a substitute for attic air sealing or a failed HVAC system—and we don’t guarantee dollar savings.

### Should I use window film or weatherstripping first?

Use both when needed: sweeps and V-strip for door/sash edge gaps; shrink film across the glass face for cold-glass rooms. Film doesn’t fix a warped exterior door. Pair film with door sweeps so you’re not sealing glass while heating the porch through a door gap.

### Is shrink film safe for bedroom windows?

Keep bedroom egress windows operable when people sleep there—don’t permanently film over the only exit. Keep film away from space-heater surfaces and open flames, and never seal dryer vents or combustion-air paths closed.

### Why does window insulation kit tape fail?

Usually dirty, damp, or silicone-contaminated frames. Clean and dry the tape landing zone (isopropyl helps on paint/vinyl), apply double-sided tape firmly, wait briefly before stretching film, and shrink with a moving hair dryer on medium heat so you don’t melt holes. Vinyl vs painted trim needs different prep—see the troubleshooting table above.

### Which pack size should I buy first?

Start with a 3-pack or 4-pack on the worst two rooms after you measure the frame perimeter where tape lands. Step up to a 9-pack once you’ve confirmed tape sticks on your trim. Use the oversized patio sheet only for measured patio doors or picture windows that exceed standard 42×62 sheets.
