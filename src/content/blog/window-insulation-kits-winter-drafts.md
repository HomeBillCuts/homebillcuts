## When shrink film helps *your* bill

It can help when:

- You feel cold air at the glass or sash, or rooms with big window walls never hold setpoint.
- Windows are **single-pane**, storm-window missing, or older double-hung with tired sash seals.
- You already fixed setpoints and dirty [HVAC filters](/blog/hvac-air-filters-merv-efficiency/) — infiltration is the next cheap lever.
- You rent or you’re waiting on a real window project and need a **seasonal** fix.

It usually won’t help much when:

- Units are already good sealed double- or triple-pane with intact weatherstrip and no sash play.
- The real driver is failed HVAC, electric strip heat thrashing, or a wide-open attic (see [attic insulation](/blog/attic-insulation-approaches-when-to-call-pro/)).
- You leave operable **egress** bedrooms sealed shut (unsafe) or block combustion-appliance air paths.

**Honest framing:** Film reduces *infiltration and some conductive loss* by creating a still air gap. Savings scale with how leaky the window was, your climate, and your rates — not with the brand on the box. We do **not** guarantee a dollar cut.

## Film vs weatherstripping (use both when needed)

| Job | Better tool |
| --- | --- |
| Gap under an exterior door | Door sweep / threshold ([weatherstripping guide](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/)) |
| Flat sash / stop leaks you can feel with incense | V-strip, foam tape, rope caulk |
| Baseboards, penetrations, exterior-wall outlets | [Caulk, foam & outlet gaskets](/blog/air-sealing-caulk-foam-outlet-gaskets/) |
| Whole window face drafts / “cold glass” rooms | Indoor shrink film kit (this page) |
| Daily open/close + light control on cold glass | [Thermal / blackout curtains](/blog/thermal-blackout-curtains-energy-savings/) |
| Failed, warped exterior door slab | Insulated exterior door (measure first) — see weatherstripping “whole door” section |

Film does **not** replace fixing a banana-shaped door. Pair film on glass with sweeps on doors when both leak.

## Safety and renter notes

> **Safety:** Keep bedroom egress windows operable when occupied for sleeping — do not permanently film over the only exit. Never seal dryer vents, range hoods, or bathroom fans closed. Do not block required combustion air for furnaces, water heaters, or fireplaces. Use the hair dryer carefully; keep film away from space-heater surfaces and open flames.

Renters: film + included tape is usually removable in spring; test a small frame area first. Keep original hardware and get landlord approval if your lease restricts adhesives on painted trim.

## Install so the tape actually holds

1. **Clean and dry** the frame where tape lands — dust and silicone residue are why kits “fail.” Isopropyl alcohol on a cloth helps on painted/varnished wood and vinyl.
2. Apply double-sided tape firmly; many installs improve if you wait **10–15 minutes** after peeling the backing before stretching film.
3. Cut film oversized; attach to tape without wrinkles at the perimeter; leave a little give if the wall flexes in wind.
4. Shrink with a hair dryer on medium heat, keep moving, and stop when the film is taut — overheating melts holes.
5. Trim excess with a sharp blade *outside* the sealed area.
6. Recheck after the first cold windy night; reinforce weak corners with clear packing tape if needed.

Basement hopper windows and window-AC side gaps can use leftover scraps — still keep required ventilation and egress in mind.

## Habits that multiply a film kit

1. Film the **worst two rooms** first (often bedrooms or a glass patio wall), then decide if the rest is worth it.
2. Pair with door sweeps from the [weatherstripping guide](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/) so you’re not sealing glass while heating the porch through a ½″ door gap.
3. Ease thermostat setbacks slightly only after drafts drop — comfort first, then setpoint ([smart thermostat guide](/blog/best-smart-thermostats-2026/)).
4. In spring, remove film carefully; scrape residual tape with a plastic scraper, not a razor on vinyl.

## Bottom line

For drafty US windows heading into heating season, indoor shrink-film kits are a **low-cost, reversible** weatherization step — not a substitute for attic air sealing or a failed HVAC system. Buy the pack size that matches how many openings you measured, install on clean dry frames, and keep egress and combustion air safe.
