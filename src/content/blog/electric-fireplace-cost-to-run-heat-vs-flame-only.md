Electric fireplaces are one of the most-bought "heat" purchases every October, and the marketing often promises big bill cuts. The honest version: **the heater is a 1,500 W space heater in a nice box, and the flame is nearly free.** Whether it saves you money depends entirely on one thing — **does it let you turn down the heat for the rest of the house?** This guide is **bill-first**: cost per hour, per evening, and per season at your rate, heat vs flame-only, a one-week measurement test, and when to buy. ## The only formula you need

**Cost = watts ÷ 1,000 × hours × your rate ($/kWh).**

Find your real rate on your bill: total supply + delivery charges ÷ kWh used. We use **$0.17/kWh** as an illustrative US average; many homes in the Northeast and California pay $0.25–$0.40.

Electric fireplaces have two very different modes:

- **Heat on (usually 1,500 W high / 750 W low).** This is where the money goes.
- **Flame-only (LED effect, typically ~10–50 W).** Ambience for pennies.

Most units have a built-in thermostat that cycles the heater once the room reaches the set temperature, so real-world draw on "high" is often less than 1,500 W every hour — which is exactly why measuring beats guessing.

Heat output is fixed by watts: **1,500 W ≈ 5,100 BTU/h**, no matter whether the box says infrared, quartz, fan-forced, or "3D flame."

## When it actually saves money: the zone-heating test

Electric resistance heat costs about **$0.050 per 1,000 BTU at $0.17/kWh**. Compare with your main system:

- **Gas furnace** at ~$1.50/therm and 95% efficiency ≈ **$0.016 per 1,000 BTU** — about a third the cost of the fireplace per unit of heat.
- **Heat pump** at a seasonal COP of ~2.5–3 ≈ **$0.017–$0.020 per 1,000 BTU** — again much cheaper per unit.
- **Electric baseboard or electric furnace** ≈ the same as the fireplace. Here zone heating is almost always a win.
- **Propane or heating oil** at high prices can land close to resistance electric — run your own numbers.

So per BTU, the fireplace usually loses. It wins only when you heat **one room instead of the whole house**. Rule of thumb from energy agencies: setting the whole-house thermostat back helps meaningfully (DOE cites up to ~10% a year for a 7–10°F setback 8 hours a day). The honest playbook:

1. Turn the central thermostat down 4–8°F during the hours you're in one room.
2. Run the fireplace's own thermostat in that room.
3. Close that room's door if you can.
4. Measure for a week (below) and compare your gas or electric usage to a similar-weather week.

If you won't turn the main heat down, **buy it for looks and use flame-only** — that's the cheap way to enjoy it.

## One-week measurement test

1. Plug the fireplace into a [Kill A Watt meter](/blog/kill-a-watt-plug-in-energy-meter/) (directly into the wall — the P4400 is rated 15 A).
2. Use it normally for 7 days. Read total kWh.
3. Multiply by your rate. ×21 ≈ a 150-day season.
4. Note your thermostat setback and check your gas/electric usage on the utility app for the same week vs a comparable week.

Hardwired or 240 V units won't fit a plug meter — use a whole-home monitor like [Emporia or Sense](/blog/emporia-vue-vs-sense/), or estimate from the nameplate.

## Free ways to cut the cost

- **Use flame-only** whenever you don't need the heat.
- **Use low (750 W)** and the built-in thermostat instead of high continuously.
- **Use the timer/auto-off** so it doesn't run after you leave the room.
- **Seal the room first:** [weatherstripping & draft stoppers](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/), [window insulation kits](/blog/window-insulation-kits-winter-drafts/), and [thermal curtains](/blog/thermal-blackout-curtains-energy-savings/) make every heater watt go further.
- **Sit close** — infrared models heat people in front of them more than the far corners.

## Buyer decision tree

- **Just want ambience?** Any model with a true flame-only mode. Check that flame-only works with heater fully off.
- **Want real supplemental heat in one room?** A 1,500 W unit with a built-in thermostat, low/high settings, timer, tip-over or overheat protection, and a UL/ETL/CSA listing.
- **Have an unused wood fireplace?** A log-set insert sits in the firebox — but block the chimney draft first, or the warm air goes up the flue.
- **Want a built-in look?** Recessed/wall-mount units — same efficiency, more install cost; confirm the outlet location or whether an electrician is needed.
- **Already have a heat pump or efficient gas furnace and won't set it back?** Skip the heater purchase; a cheaper [oil-filled heater](/blog/oil-filled-space-heaters-zone-heating/) or [heated throw](/blog/electric-blanket-heated-throw-cost-to-run/) may be better zone heat per dollar.

## Safety do-nots

- **Don't use extension cords, power strips, or ordinary smart plugs** — plug the heater straight into the wall.
- **Keep the manufacturer's clearances** from curtains, bedding, and furniture; don't block the vents.
- **Don't leave it heating unattended or while sleeping** unless the manual explicitly allows it.
- **Don't mount a TV directly above a heat vent** without checking both manuals.
- **Don't trust "cuts your bill 50%" claims** — savings come from setting back your main heat, not from the box.

## Honest limits
