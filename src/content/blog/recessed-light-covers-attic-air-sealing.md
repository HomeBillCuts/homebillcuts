Warm indoor air rises through the housing and trim gaps; cellulose and fiberglass get shoved aside so the fixture does not overheat — and those cleared bowls become **permanent attic-floor leaks**. **Fire-safe recessed light covers** (Tenmat FF130E-class mineral-wool domes, Battic-class ABS air-sealing covers, and similar listed products) let you restore a continuous air and insulation plane over the cans — without cardboard hacks and without inventing dollar savings for your address.

## Honest framing: attic-floor holes with a fire rule attached

Recessed lights are not just “drafts.” They are **electrical fixtures that get hot**, with thermal protectors, lamp limits, and **IC vs non-IC** rules that decide whether insulation may touch the housing.

**Covers help most when:**

- You have older **non-IC** or leaky cans with insulation pulled back into bowls above bedrooms or hallways.
- You feel cold trim rings or smell attic air at the can in winter.
- You’re about to [blow or top-dress attic insulation](/blog/attic-insulation-approaches-when-to-call-pro/) and don’t want to leave dozens of bypasses in the floor.
- Pull-down stairs or the hatch already have (or will get) a [stair/hatch cover](/blog/attic-stair-hatch-insulation-covers/), and cans are the next ceiling-plane leak.
- You’ve already knocked out easier wins on the [checklist](/blog/lower-electric-bill-priority-checklist/) (filters, setpoints, door drafts).

**Covers help less (or the wrong thing) when:**

- Cans are already **IC-AT / airtight** remodel or new-construction housings with continuous insulation and tight trims.
- The real plume is the [attic stair](/blog/attic-stair-hatch-insulation-covers/), a chase, or leaking ducts — sequence those first.
- Fixtures are damaged, scorched, or cycling off — that’s an **electrician** path, not an Amazon dome.
- You “seal” with cardboard, grocery bags, or foam packed against the can.

**Honest framing:** Agency and utility writeups often call recessed lights a major ceiling bypass family. Treat that as motivation to **inspect and seal safely**, not as a promised percent off your bill. Climate, rates, lamp type, how many cans you have, and whether bigger holes still roar decide the outcome. We do **not** guarantee savings.

## Measure-first walk: count cans, read IC labels, decide (before you buy)

Ordering a 20-pack before you know how many non-IC cans sit under the insulation is how you get dusty returns. Do this once **before** you click Buy:

1. **Kill power at the breaker** for the rooms you’ll open — treat every can as live until proven otherwise. Use a non-contact tester if you handle trims.
2. **Count cans from below and above** — walk every finished ceiling with recessed lights, then the attic bays over those rooms. Photograph clusters (kitchen rows, hall runs, bath cans).
3. **Read IC / non-IC labels** — from the attic, find the housing stamp or label: **IC**, **ICAT / Air-Tite**, or no rating. Write the model. No label → treat as **non-IC** until a pro confirms.
4. **Note lamp type and heat** — still running high-wattage incandescent or halogen? Prefer LED lamps within the cover’s listed limits, or plan a housing swap. Hot cans + buried insulation is the failure mode covers exist to prevent.
5. **Measure fixture envelope vs cover size** — Tenmat FF130E-class domes are roughly **15″ × 14″ inside footprint × ~9″ tall** (confirm the listing). Check joist spacing (often 16″ O.C.), nearby ducts, and whether a can sits against a joist (covers can often be cut for wires/joists per instructions).
6. **Check attic access and insulation plan** — will you top-dress after covers? Leave flags at every can so a blow doesn’t hide an unfinished fixture. Pair with [attic insulation sequencing](/blog/attic-insulation-approaches-when-to-call-pro/).
7. **Safety pass** — scorch marks, melted wire nuts, open junction boxes, knob-and-tube, damp stains, or pest nests → **stop and call an electrician / remediator**. Keep a spotter, dust PPE, and board-safe footing on joists.
8. **Decide cover vs remodel vs skip** — sound labeled cans → fire-safe covers (+ base sealant the maker allows). Bad/unknown cans or a ceiling remodel → airtight IC-AT housing or listed wafer via electrician. Already sealed IC-AT → skip.

Wrong count or wrong IC assumption = wasted money or a fire risk. Measure and photograph first.

## Situation priorities + do-not list

### Prioritize (biggest comfort + bill leverage)

1. **Non-IC cans with insulation scooped into bowls** over living spaces — classic winter bypass and thermal-protector cycling risk.
2. **Leaky IC cans** that still whistle at the trim or show daylight into the attic — cover + base seal, or airtight remodel if the housing itself is junk.
3. **Cans you’re about to bury under new attic fill** — cover/seal *before* or while you top-dress so you don’t leave chimneys in the floor ([attic insulation](/blog/attic-insulation-approaches-when-to-call-pro/)).
4. **Hall and bedroom clusters** you feel every night — after the [attic stair/hatch](/blog/attic-stair-hatch-insulation-covers/) is handled.
5. **Cold-climate stack-effect homes** where ceiling plane leaks dump heat all night.

### Lower priority / skip for now

- Already airtight **IC-AT** housings with continuous insulation and quiet trims.
- Obsessing over two guest-room cans while the [pull-down stair](/blog/attic-stair-hatch-insulation-covers/) still dumps hallway air.
- Decorative remodel for looks alone when the bill driver is filters, setpoints, or empty attic bays ([checklist](/blog/lower-electric-bill-priority-checklist/)).
- Exterior porch cans that never touch the conditioned attic floor plane.

### Do-not list (fire + code + wasted money)

- **Never bury non-IC cans** in cellulose, fiberglass, or foam — use a listed cover or replace the housing.
- **No cardboard, newspaper, grocery bags, or random plastic buckets** as “covers.”
- **Don’t foam-pack the can itself** or fill the lamp cavity — seal the **cover-to-drywall** perimeter with maker-allowed fire-rated sealant, not a spray-foam tomb around a hot housing.
- **Don’t ignore thermal protectors cycling** (lights that click off when buried) — that’s a heat warning, not a thermostat quirk.
- **Don’t use covers in 1–2 hour fire-rated ceiling assemblies** unless the specific product listing allows it (Tenmat FF130E materials commonly exclude those assemblies — read the sheet).
- **Don’t skip lamp wattage / type limits** printed for the cover and the can.
- **Don’t work live** — breaker off; respect aluminum wiring and damaged boxes by calling a pro.
- **Don’t invent guaranteed bill cuts** from a product title or a DOE percentage quote.

## Buyer decision tree (cover vs airtight remodel vs wafer vs pro vs skip)

Walk the tree in order:

1. **Do recessed cans penetrate a vented attic floor over conditioned space?**  
   No (cathedral sealed assemblies, already canless wafers, no attic) → stop; this guide isn’t your lever.  
   Yes → continue.

2. **Any scorch, melting, open splices, or unknown old wiring?**  
   Yes → **electrician first**. Covers don’t fix unsafe electrical.  
   Sound equipment → continue.

3. **Can you read IC / IC-AT / non-IC on each housing type?**  
   No → treat as non-IC; photograph and either cover with a listed product that allows non-IC within wattage limits, or call an electrician to identify/replace.  
   Yes → continue.

4. **Goal is keep the cans and restore attic-floor air seal + insulation continuity?**  
   Yes → **fire-safe listed covers** (Tenmat FF130E-class for most DIY attic jobs; Battic-class ABS only where the maker allows your IC fixture). Count cans; buy singles to test-fit, then a 6- or 20-pack.  
   Want factory-airtight new housings from below → continue.

5. **Ceiling remodel or chronically bad non-IC cans?**  
   Yes → **airtight IC-AT remodel housings** or **listed canless/wafer LEDs** (typically electrician). Confirm IC + airtight ratings on the cut sheet.  
   No → stick with covers.

6. **Already IC-AT, insulated over, no draft at trim?**  
   Yes → **skip**. Put budget into [stair covers](/blog/attic-stair-hatch-insulation-covers/), [air sealing](/blog/air-sealing-caulk-foam-outlet-gaskets/), or depth.  
   Still drafty → cover base seal, trim gasket, or housing replacement — not more fluffy insulation alone.

7. **Still unsure after the walk?**  
   Cover the worst cluster with one verified Tenmat-class dome, live a cold week, feel the trim, then finish the attic — or book a home-performance / electrical pro for a bulk swap.

## Honest limits (what a can cover will not fix)

- **Unsafe electrical** — scorched cans, bad splices, and mystery wiring need a licensed electrician.
- **Guaranteed** heating-bill cuts or a universal “percent of ceiling loss” as your personal result.
- A **pull-down stair or thin hatch** that still dumps hallway air ([stair covers](/blog/attic-stair-hatch-insulation-covers/)).
- **Empty joist bays and duct leaks** — covers seal fixtures; they don’t add R-value to bare bays ([attic insulation](/blog/attic-insulation-approaches-when-to-call-pro/)).
- **Fire-rated ceiling assemblies** the specific cover listing excludes.
- **Moisture, ice dams, or pests** that need remediation before envelope cosmetics.
- **Combustion / CO** problems — detectors and licensed service, not mineral wool domes.
- A **whole-house fan shutter** or other ceiling hole you left open ([fan covers](/blog/whole-house-fan-winter-covers/)).

## Post-setup checklist + habits

1. Breakers off for work zones; spotter + boards + dust PPE in the attic.
2. Label log: room, count, IC vs non-IC photo, lamp type.
3. Test-fit one cover; cut only as the instructions allow for wires/joists.
4. Seat every dome; seal the **skirt to drywall** with allowed fire-rated caulk.
5. Restore continuous insulation **over** the covers (don’t leave bowls).
6. Restore power; run lamps 30+ minutes; watch for cycling, smell, or discoloration.
7. Cold-week trim check downstairs; reseal any whistlers.
8. Sequence siblings: [stair/hatch cover](/blog/attic-stair-hatch-insulation-covers/), other [air sealing](/blog/air-sealing-caulk-foam-outlet-gaskets/), then [insulation depth](/blog/attic-insulation-approaches-when-to-call-pro/) if bays are still thin.

### Habits that multiply (or replace) a cover purchase

- Swap remaining high-wattage incandescent cans to **listed LED lamps** within cover/housing limits — less heat, less protector drama.
- Seal the **attic stair** the same weekend; cans + hatch is a common pair.
- Don’t blow cellulose until cans are covered or clearly IC-AT with continuous fill planned.
- Keep one spare cover; remodel dust and pests tear mineral wool.
- Track comfort (cold spots) separately from kWh fantasies — covers can be worth it as safety + draft control even when the meter barely moves.
