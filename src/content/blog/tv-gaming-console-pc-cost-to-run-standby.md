## The only formula you need

**Cost = watts ÷ 1,000 × hours × your rate ($/kWh).**

Find your rate on your bill: total supply + delivery charges ÷ kWh used. We use **$0.17/kWh** as an illustrative US average below; yours may be well above or below.

Two kinds of hours matter:

- **Active hours** — screen on, game running, PC working.
- **Idle/standby hours** — everything else. That's usually 16–24 hours a day, so a small idle draw multiplies fast: **1 W around the clock ≈ 8.8 kWh a year ≈ $1.50.** A 15 W idle draw ≈ $22 a year.

These are ranges drawn from manufacturer specs and typical meter readings, not a lab test. Your model, settings, and habits decide your number — which is why the next section exists.

## Measure first: the one-week plug-meter test

1. **Group the cluster.** Put everything in the entertainment center (or desk) on one strip.
2. **Plug the strip into a plug-in energy meter** like a Kill A Watt, and reset its kWh counter.
3. **Live normally for 7 days.** Don't change habits — you want the real baseline.
4. **Read kWh and hours.** kWh ÷ 7 × 365 × rate = yearly cost.
5. **Measure idle separately.** Late at night with everything "off," read live watts. That number × 8,760 h ÷ 1,000 × rate is your standby bill.
6. **Unplug devices one at a time** while watching live watts to find the offender (often the console, receiver, sub, or cable box).

If you'd rather leave something in place permanently, an energy-monitoring smart plug or the Kasa HS300 strip reports per-outlet watts in an app. Accuracy is fine for relative decisions, not utility-grade billing.

## Free settings that cut kWh (do these before buying anything)

- **TV picture mode:** switch from Vivid/Dynamic/Store to Standard, Movie, or Filmmaker mode; lower backlight in a dim room. Turn on the auto-brightness / ambient light sensor if offered.
- **TV "quick start" / instant-on:** turning it off restores true sub-watt standby on many sets, at the cost of a few seconds of boot time.
- **Console power mode:** choose energy-saving standby instead of rest/instant-on if you don't need background downloads or voice wake. Set auto-off after inactivity (e.g., 1 hour) and controller auto-off.
- **PC sleep:** set sleep after 15–30 minutes idle and display off after 5–10. Check that sleep actually happens (some peripherals and apps block it). A PC left mining, rendering, or downloading overnight is a choice — price it.
- **Frame-rate cap / V-sync:** capping frames at your monitor's refresh rate can cut GPU power during play without a visible difference.
- **Streaming on a console vs. a stick:** a streaming stick or the TV's built-in app typically draws a few watts versus a console's tens of watts for the same show.
- **HDMI-CEC:** set it so the TV turning off also turns off the soundbar/receiver — or confirm it's not waking everything back up.
- **Cable box:** ask about eco/low-power modes or a newer model.

## Buyer decision tree

- **Haven't measured?** → Buy a **plug-in meter** first (about the cost of a pizza). Skip everything else until you know idle watts.
- **Cluster idles under ~5 W?** → Stop. You're done; there's no money left to chase here.
- **Idle 5–20 W and you'll actually flip a switch?** → A plain switched strip you turn off at bedtime is enough.
- **Idle 20 W+ or you won't remember?** → A **smart strip with individually switched outlets** (keep the router/streaming box on, schedule the console, receiver, and sub off overnight).
- **One big offender (old receiver, DVR, PC that won't sleep)?** → A single **energy-monitoring smart plug** on a schedule, or fix the device's settings.
- **Gaming PC is the big number?** → Fix sleep, cap frames, and accept that play hours cost what they cost. Don't buy a new PSU or GPU "to save power" — the payback is usually many years.
- **Thinking about a new TV to save energy?** → Only replace a working set if it's an old plasma or CCFL LCD that meters 200 W+. Compare the yellow EnergyGuide label yearly cost on new models.

## Do-nots

- **Don't cut power to your router, modem, or a DVR mid-recording** on a schedule unless you've accepted the trade-off.
- **Don't hard-cut a console during a system update** — schedule outages for times it's idle, or use its own energy-saving mode instead.
- **Don't daisy-chain power strips** or overload a strip; respect the total amp rating.
- **Don't plug space heaters into smart strips or smart plugs** meant for electronics.
- **Don't buy new hardware purely to save electricity** without doing the payback math first.

## Honest limits

- **Our numbers are illustrative.** Models vary widely; your meter reading wins.
- **Plug meters can't see hardwired or 240 V loads,** and cheap smart-plug meters are approximate.
- **Standby savings are real but small per device.** The win comes from a cluster or one bad actor.
- **In winter, electronics' waste heat isn't totally wasted** — it slightly offsets heating; in summer your AC has to remove it.
- **We don't guarantee any savings.**
