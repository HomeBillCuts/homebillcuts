An overhead garage door is a giant movable wall. When the **bottom rubber is cracked, the side stop molding is gaping, or the slab is uneven**, cold air, rain, leaves, and pests pour in — and in an **attached garage**, that air often migrates through the garage-to-house door into living space. **Bottom T/bulb seals, side & top jamb weatherstrip, and floor threshold kits** are the usual DIY stack for heating season — without inventing dollar savings for your address.

## When garage door seals actually move your bill

Think of the closed garage door as part of your home’s weather shell when the garage is **attached**. Stack effect and wind pressure pull outdoor air through any continuous gap at the bottom rail or jambs; that air then loads the shared wall, the garage-to-house door, and sometimes the living space beyond. Sealing the big door is cheap envelope work — but only when the door actually seats and the leak is real.

Sealing helps most when:

- You see **daylight under or beside** the closed door, or feel wind at the bottom on a breezy day.
- The garage is **attached** and the house entry door from the garage is already weatherstripped — garage envelope leaks still load that shared wall and the living space beyond.
- Heating or cooling equipment sits in the garage, or you condition the garage itself.
- The existing bottom seal is **hard, torn, flattened, or missing**, or side stop molding no longer touches the door face.
- You park a warm car inside and notice the house entry still feels cold — garage air is mixing.

Sealing helps less when:

- The real problem is a **failed opener, binding tracks, or a warped door** that never seats — seals can’t fix mechanical fit.
- You’re only sealing a **detached** shed you never heat or cool (comfort/pest yes; bill impact usually tiny).
- Moisture or flood risk needs a **threshold + drainage plan**, not just a thin wiper.
- You expect a bottom seal alone to match a full insulation retrofit of the house ([attic insulation](/blog/attic-insulation-approaches-when-to-call-pro/)).
- The garage-to-house door is the bigger leak and you haven’t touched its sweep yet ([weatherstripping](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/)).

**Honest framing:** Garage weatherization reduces infiltration and keeps the garage cleaner/drier. Bill impact scales with how leaky the door was, whether the garage is attached, your climate, and your rates — not with the brand printed on the rubber. We do **not** guarantee a dollar cut.

## Measure first: walk the door before you buy

Wrong profile = wasted shipping and a door that still leaks. Spend ten minutes with a flashlight and a tape before you click “Buy.”

1. **Close the door fully** (opener locked out / power disconnected if you’ll work under it — see safety). Look for daylight along the bottom and both jambs. A dollar bill or paper strip that slides freely under the closed door is a clear gap signal.
2. **Identify the bottom retainer** — most modern steel sectionals use an aluminum channel that accepts a **T-end** rubber (common bead widths **1/4″** and **5/16″**). Older wood doors may use nail-on bulbs or need a **new aluminum retainer** screwed to the bottom rail. Photograph the bead end-on.
3. **Measure door width** and buy seal length with **1–2 ft of extra** to trim. Single-car openings are often ~8–10 ft; double-car ~16–18 ft of opening width (kits are sold longer so you cut to fit).
4. **Check floor flatness** — big dips, a crowned slab, or an inward-sloped apron may need a **bulb/U+O style** bottom seal and/or a **floor-mounted PVC threshold**, not a skinny flat wiper. Note low spots with chalk.
5. **Measure side + top perimeter** (both jambs + header) before ordering stop molding kits (25 ft kits suit many singles; 30–36 ft kits for doubles). Include a little waste for mitered corners.
6. **Photograph the T-bead cross-section** of the old rubber so you match 1/4″ vs 5/16″ — wrong bead = won’t slide or falls out. If the retainer is crushed or missing segments, plan a **retainer + rubber kit** instead of rubber-only.
7. **Check the garage-to-house door** on the same walk — a perfect overhead seal still loses if the man-door sweep is shot.
8. **Note water paths** — staining on the slab edge, leaf piles inside the threshold line, or salt tracks after storms argue for a threshold after (or with) the bottom rubber.

Write the bead size, opening width, and “daylight? Y/N” on a sticky note before you open Amazon.

## Situation priorities + do-not-seal safety list

### Prioritize (biggest comfort + bill leverage)

- **Attached garage**, daylight under the closed door, and a flattened or missing bottom seal — replace bottom rubber first.
- **Attached garage** with daylight at jambs/header after the bottom is healthy — add side/top stop molding.
- **Water, pests, or a large remaining floor gap** after rubber upgrade — add a floor threshold sized to clear door travel.
- **Shared wall / house entry still drafty** — weatherstrip the garage-to-house door the same weekend ([weatherstripping guide](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/)).
- **Heating equipment in the garage** — sealing the overhead door cuts cold infiltration that the system then fights; keep combustion make-up air honest (see safety).

### Lower priority / skip for now

- Detached, never-conditioned sheds used only for storage — pest/dust seals are fine; don’t expect a utility-bill story.
- Cosmetic gaps on a door that already compresses well with no daylight and no wind feel.
- Fancy brush seals or specialty profiles before a basic T-end match is confirmed.
- Full door replacement when a $30–$80 seal kit would close the visible gap.

### Do-not-seal / do-not-DIY safety list

- **Never adjust torsion springs, stretch extension springs, or cut cables** unless you are trained. Seal work stays on the bottom rail and jamb stops.
- **Don’t defeat photo-eyes or auto-reverse** to force a thick seal or tall threshold — crushing force kills. If the door binds, trim/adjust the seal or call a tech.
- **Don’t work under a live opener** — disconnect power and use the emergency release; prop or clamp if you must be underneath.
- **Don’t seal every combustion-air path** if a furnace or water heater lives in the garage. Door sealing is fine; blocking dedicated make-up air or flues is not. Keep **CO alarms** working on living floors.
- **Don’t ignore an off-track door, frayed cables, or an opener that struggles** — fix the door first, then seal.
- **Don’t caulk the door to the floor** as a “permanent” fix — overhead doors must travel; use designed seals and thresholds only.

If any item on the do-not list applies, pause the Amazon cart and call a garage-door tech for the mechanical problem.

## Product types at a glance

| Type | Best when | Limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **T-end bottom rubber** (slide-in) | Existing aluminum retainer in good shape; measured 1/4″ or 5/16″ T-slot | Won’t fit bead/J retainers; uneven floors may still show gaps |
| **U+O / bulb-style bottom insert** | Want more compression on imperfect slabs | Must match your retainer; thicker seals can bind if tracks are tight |
| **Bottom seal + aluminum retainer kit** | Retainer is bent, missing, or wood door with flat bottom | More install time; drill carefully; re-check opener force/photo-eyes |
| **Side & top stop weatherstrip** | Daylight at jambs/header; old vinyl stop is brittle | Cold PVC can snap — warm before uncoiling; nail into solid jamb |
| **Floor threshold seal** | Water intrusion, pests, or large bottom gap after rubber upgrade | Adhesive cure time; height must clear door travel; not a flood wall by itself |

**Rule of thumb:** replace cracked bottom rubber first → add side/top stop if jambs gap → add threshold only if water/pests or floor geometry still wins.

## Buyer decision tree (bottom rubber vs kit vs stop vs threshold vs pro)

Use this as a quick filter — then buy only what your measure-first walk supports.

1. **Daylight or wind under the closed door?**  
   - Yes → go to step 2.  
   - No, but jambs/header show light → skip to side/top stop.  
   - No visible leaks → stop; check the house entry door and attic bypasses instead.
2. **Is the aluminum T-retainer intact and straight?**  
   - Yes → buy **slide-in T-end** rubber matching **1/4″ or 5/16″** bead (U+O/bulb if the slab is uneven).  
   - No / missing / wood flat bottom → buy a **bottom seal + aluminum retainer kit**.
3. **After bottom rubber, do jambs or header still show daylight?**  
   - Yes → add a **side & top stop kit** sized to your perimeter (25 / 30 / 36 ft).  
   - No → skip stop molding for now.
4. **Water, pests, or a remaining floor gap after rubber?**  
   - Yes → add a **floor threshold** that clears door travel; follow adhesive cure times.  
   - No → you’re done with the overhead door envelope for this weekend.
5. **Door off-track, springs scary, cables frayed, or opener binds after a proper seal?**  
   - Yes → **pro**. Seal kits don’t replace hardware.

**Garage seals vs other draft fixes:** Overhead door seals close a huge movable hole. They don’t replace [weatherstripping](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/) on the man-door, [air sealing](/blog/air-sealing-caulk-foam-outlet-gaskets/) on shared walls, [window film](/blog/window-insulation-kits-winter-drafts/) upstairs, or [attic stair covers](/blog/attic-stair-hatch-insulation-covers/). Stack them in that cheap-to-expensive order when the checklist says so.

## Install / troubleshooting table

| Symptom | Likely cause | What to try |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rubber won’t slide into the channel | Wrong T-bead width, dry/dirty channel, bent retainer | Confirm 1/4″ vs 5/16″; soap the bead (no petroleum oil); straighten or replace retainer |
| Corners still show daylight after new bottom seal | Seal trimmed short, uneven slab, missing end plugs | Recut square with slight compression; add end plugs; consider U+O profile or threshold |
| Door binds or opener reverses mid-close | Seal too thick, threshold too tall, tracks tight | Trim seal height; lower threshold choice; never raise force limits as the “fix” |
| Side stop won’t stay against the door | Nails in soft trim only; PVC cold-snapped; stop too far from face | Nail into solid jamb; warm PVC before uncoiling; set a light wipe, not a crush |
| Water still sheets in after rubber upgrade | Slope toward garage, no threshold, damaged apron | Add threshold; improve exterior drainage; rubber alone isn’t a flood barrier |
| Photo-eye light out or door won’t close | Eyes blocked/misaligned after work | Clean lenses; realign to same height; test auto-reverse with a 2×4 on the floor |
| New retainer screws strip or pull out | Soft wood bottom rail or wrong pilot holes | Use correct length screws into solid material; don’t over-torque thin sheet metal |
| Seal works in summer, gaps in winter | Rubber hardened, PVC shrunk, slab heaved | Replace aged EPDM; recheck winter compression; threshold for seasonal heave |

## Safety first (non-negotiable)

> **Read this before working under or around a powered garage door.**

- **Disconnect opener power** (unplug or breaker) and use the **emergency release** so the door can’t move while you’re under it. Prop or clamp the door open if you must work underneath — never trust a spring alone.
- **Springs and cables are dangerous.** Don’t adjust torsion springs, stretch extension springs, or cut cables unless you are trained. Seal work stays on the bottom rail and jamb stops.
- **Photo-eye sensors** must stay aligned and unobstructed after you add seals or thresholds. Test auto-reverse: the door should reverse when it hits a 2×4 on the floor (per opener manual).
- **Don’t defeat auto-reverse** to “make a thick seal fit.” If the door binds, trim/adjust the seal or call a tech — crushing force kills.
- **Combustion appliances in the garage** (furnace, water heater) need make-up air and clear vents. Sealing the door is fine; sealing every combustion-air path is not. Keep **CO alarms** working on living floors.
- **Ladder / pinch awareness** — side stop nailing is overhead work; wear eye protection when cutting aluminum or PVC.
- **After install:** reconnect power, run several open/close cycles, re-check sensor lights, and listen for scraping.

If the door is off-track, cables are frayed, or the opener struggles, fix the door first — then seal.

## Install habits that keep seals working

1. **Match the T-bead** before peeling packaging — return unopened wrong sizes.
2. **Soap the bead**, slide from one end, and seat end plugs if included.
3. **Trim square**; leave a slight compression on the floor without dragging the opener.
4. Warm PVC stop molding before bending corners; nail from the center outward.
5. After any bottom or threshold change, **test photo-eyes and auto-reverse**.
6. Pair with a solid [garage-to-house door sweep](/blog/best-weatherstripping-draft-stoppers/) — sealing the big door while the house door leaks still wastes heat.
7. Recheck compression after the first hard freeze — rubber and slabs move with temperature.
8. Keep a spare bottom seal length if your climate eats EPDM every few winters.

## What garage seals won’t replace

## FAQ

### Will garage door weather seals lower my heating bill?

They help most when you see daylight under or beside a closed door — especially on an **attached** garage where cold air migrates toward living space. Bill impact scales with leakiness, climate, and rates. We do **not** guarantee a dollar cut. Detached unconditioned sheds rarely move the electric or gas bill.

### What should I replace first — bottom seal, side stops, or a threshold?

Replace cracked, flattened, or missing **bottom rubber** first. Add **side and top stop molding** if jambs/header still show daylight. Add a **floor threshold** mainly for water, pests, or large bottom gaps that rubber alone cannot fix. Seals cannot fix a warped door or bad tracks.

### How do I match a T-end bottom seal to my door?

Identify the aluminum retainer bead — common T widths are **1/4 inch** and **5/16 inch** — and photograph the old rubber cross-section. Measure door width and buy extra length to trim. Wrong bead size will jam or fall out; uneven floors may need a thicker U+O/bulb profile or a threshold.

### Is it safe to install garage door seals myself?

Yes for bottom rail and jamb stops if you **disconnect opener power**, never adjust springs/cables, and re-test photo-eyes and auto-reverse afterward. Don’t defeat safety reverse to force a thick seal. Keep combustion make-up air and CO alarms in mind if appliances live in the garage.

### Should I seal the garage door or weatherstrip the house first?

Do both if both leak — but if you can only do one evening, close the **biggest continuous gap** first (often daylight under the overhead door on an attached garage) and weatherstrip the **garage-to-house door** the same weekend.
